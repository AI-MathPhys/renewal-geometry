\begin{abstract}
This monograph extracts the stable mathematical core of the cumulative renewal--arithmetic programme into one standalone document.  The basic object is not the original one-variable shifted-correlation integral, whose zero-shift slice contains coefficients of type $\Lambda(n)^2$ and is therefore not an $L$-function frame.  The corrected object is an anchored, bi-reflected, normal--anomalous port tensor, together with its source-to-future Schur quotient.  Its anchor retains linear amplitudes; Fourier charge conjugation separates difference and sum constraints; Mellin reflection retains horizontal zero information; and Schur shorting removes scalar, diagonal, and record-erased nuisance directions before positivity is used.

Exact theorems in the monograph recover Dirichlet and logarithmic-derivative ports, shifted-prime and Goldbach marginals, finite reflected Weil forms, ordered-factor prime projectors, source-conditioned Gaussian kernels, and the canonical mass--prefix-current descent.  These results are then assembled into rigorous \emph{conditional chains}: a cutoff-matched subcritical Gaussian estimate implies RH or GRH; a positive $P_1\cup P_2$ carrier together with a selector-stable M\"obius-current estimate implies twin primes or binary Goldbach; and a common mass--prefix packet controls any preassigned finite family of spectral and additive ports.

The V002 expansion incorporates the type-correct primitive functional equation, exact imprimitive Euler corrections, a compactly supported twisted Mellin packet whose subpower growth implies Dirichlet GRH, and a separate PSWF compression route.  The latter is kept rigorously distinct from full Weil positivity: an exact radical-to-window leakage identity and a cluster--Feshbach theorem reduce it to graph-norm leakage and internal simple-even ordering.  A scalar-invariant two-channel Weyl theorem then records the critical-scale firewall: a leading positive scalar bulk carries no arithmetic spectral ordering.



The V003 expansion adds the degree-resolved Goldbach--twin-prime machinery from the oriented two-form programme.  A squarefree Mellin product is identified exactly with generalized von Mangoldt degrees; its first two Taylor coefficients split into a prime atom and a squarefree-semiprime bulk.  A signed degree-three correction cancels the entire semiprime layer pointwise, while a bounded finite Cauchy bank realizes the positive $P_1\cup P_2$ carrier inside the phase-complete tensor.  Quantitative Li--Liu prime-or-semiprime inputs are separated from an explicitly labelled support-retaining audit hypothesis.  The resulting chains distinguish four logically different routes: signed degree separation, M\"obius cancellation on a positive $P_1\cup P_2$ carrier, semiprime-profile contraction, and the classical Hardy--Littlewood asymptotic.  First-moment Maynard data, local singular-series optimization, and positive/negative Mellin-temperature comparisons are proved insufficient by themselves.

The same operator anatomy also appears in renewal spacetime and internal gauge reconstruction.  We prove the finite Clifford and qutrit algebra statements, then formulate conditional chains from retained Store--Read histories to a Lorentzian Dirac scaling limit, horizon thermodynamics, Einstein closure, and Pati--Salam-to-Standard-Model gauge descent.  These parallels are architectural, not logical implications between number theory and physics.  No proof of RH, GRH, Goldbach, twin primes, the Standard Model, or Einstein dynamics is claimed.  Every conclusion is labelled as exact, external, conditional, or open, and every conditional theorem lists the precise missing input.
\end{abstract}

\section{What is unified and what is not}

The word \emph{unified} is used in a precise operator sense.  The same finite source may be interrogated by several preassigned ports, and these ports may be placed in one positive source-to-future Gram tensor.  Difference constraints, sum constraints, critical-line reflection, factor-depth grading, and physical Store--Read records then appear as typed projections of that tensor.  A common Schur short removes a common nuisance algebra.

This does \emph{not} imply that an estimate in one sector automatically proves an estimate in another.  A reflected Mellin inequality is an order-one spectral statement, whereas a prime-pair lower bound is an order-two additive statement.  The Standard-Model and spacetime chains require physical representation and scaling hypotheses that are absent from the arithmetic tensor.

\begin{theorem}[Direct-sum firewall]\label{thm:direct-sum-firewall}
Let $\mathcal H=\mathcal H_1\oplus\mathcal H_2$ and let $T=T_1\oplus T_2\succeq0$.  Any coercive statement involving only the compression of $T$ to $\mathcal H_1$ is compatible with an arbitrary positive semidefinite operator on $\mathcal H_2$.  Consequently, positivity or coercivity of one typed block of a grand tensor cannot, without an intertwining theorem, force coercivity of another typed block.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $T_1$ satisfying the proposed estimate and choose any $T_2\succeq0$, including a singular or zero operator.  The compression of $T$ to $\mathcal H_1$ equals $T_1$ and is independent of $T_2$.  Therefore no conclusion about $T_2$ follows from the first compression alone.
\end{proof}

\section{Audit of the original one-variable tensor}

For coefficient sequences $a_i,a_j$ and fixed integer $h$, consider
\[
 A_{ij,h}(x)=\sum_{\substack{n\le x\\n+h\ge1}}a_i(n)a_j(n+h)
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb T^{\rm old}_{ij}(s,h)
 =\int_1^\infty A_{ij,h}(x)x^{-s-1}\dd x.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Abel reduction]\label{prop:old-abel}
Assume $s\ne0$ and
\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}\abs{a_i(n)a_j(n+h)}n^{-\Re s}<\infty.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb T^{\rm old}_{ij}(s,h)
 =\frac1s\sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\n+h\ge1}}
 a_i(n)a_j(n+h)n^{-s}.}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Absolute convergence permits Fubini.  The summand indexed by $n$ contributes for $x\ge n$, and
\[
 \int_n^\infty x^{-s-1}\dd x=\frac{n^{-s}}s.
\]
Summing over $n$ proves the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The zero-shift slice is not an $L$-function frame]\label{cor:old-zero}
For $a_i(n)=\Lambda(n)\rho_i(n)$,
\[
 \mathbb T^{\rm old}_{ij}(s,0)
 =\frac1s\sum_{n\ge1}\Lambda(n)^2\rho_i(n)\rho_j(n)n^{-s}.
\]
This is neither $L(s,\rho_i)$ nor $-L'/L(s,\rho_i)$.  Without conjugating one coefficient it is not even a Hermitian Gram entry.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Set $h=0$ in \Cref{prop:old-abel}.  The coefficient is $\Lambda(n)^2\rho_i(n)\rho_j(n)$, whereas a Hermitian Gram would contain $a_i(n)\overline{a_j(n)}$.
\end{proof}

\AuditBox{The corrected object needs four ingredients absent from the old tensor: an anchor for linear amplitudes, a normal and anomalous Fourier pair for difference and sum constraints, critical-line Mellin reflection, and a source-to-future quotient that removes nuisance directions before coercivity is tested.}

\chapter{The anchored bi-reflected affine port tensor}

\section{Finite-window ports}

Let $\mathscr A$ be a finite typed port set.  For each $\alpha\in\mathscr A$, choose coefficients $a_\alpha(n)\in\C$, and let $w_X$ be finitely supported.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:port}
 F_{\alpha,X}(s,\theta)
 :=\sum_{n\ge1}a_\alpha(n)w_X(n)n^{-s}\e^{2\pi i n\theta},
 \qquad (s,\theta)\in\C\times\Torus.
\end{equation}
Adjoin an anchor port $\star$ by $F_{\star,X}=1$.

For a Dirichlet character $\chi$, extended by zero away from the units, the basic ports are
\[
 a_{\chi,L}(n)=\chi(n),\qquad
 a_{\chi,P}(n)=\chi(n)\Lambda(n),\qquad
 a_{\chi,\partial L}(n)=\chi(n)\log n.
\]
After removing the window and for $\Re s>1$,
\[
 F_{\chi,L}(s,0)=L(s,\chi),\qquad
 F_{\chi,P}(s,0)=-\frac{L'}{L}(s,\chi),\qquad
 F_{\chi,\partial L}(s,0)=-L'(s,\chi).
\]

Define Fourier charge conjugation and Mellin reflection by
\[
 (\mathcal CF)(s,\theta)=\overline{F(\bar s,\theta)},
 \qquad
 (\mathcal MF)(s,\theta)=F(1-\bar s,\theta).
\]
They commute and square to the identity.  Write
\[
 \mathfrak K=\{\Id,\mathcal C,\mathcal M,\mathcal M\mathcal C\}.
\]

\begin{definition}[Kinematic grand port tensor]\label{def:port-tensor}
Order the vector
\[
 \bm\Phi_X(s,\theta)
 =\left(1,\bigl(gF_{\alpha,X}(s,\theta)\bigr)_{\alpha\in\mathscr A,\,g\in\mathfrak K}\right)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The anchored bi-reflected affine port tensor is
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb T_X^{\BR}(s,\theta)
 =\bm\Phi_X(s,\theta)\bm\Phi_X(s,\theta)^*.}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Positivity and anchor recovery]\label{prop:anchor}
For every $(s,\theta)$, $\mathbb T_X^{\BR}(s,\theta)\succeq0$ and has rank at most one.  Its cross-entry against the anchor recovers each port value $F_{\alpha,X}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The tensor is an outer product.  The $(\alpha,\star)$ entry equals $F_{\alpha,X}\overline{1}=F_{\alpha,X}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Normal and anomalous affine marginals}

For real $\sigma$, define
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal D_{\alpha\beta,X}(h;\sigma)
 &:={\int_0^1}F_{\alpha,X}(\sigma,\theta)
 \overline{F_{\beta,X}(\sigma,\theta)}\e^{-2\pi i h\theta}\dd\theta,\\
 \mathcal H_{\alpha\beta,X}(N;\sigma)
 &:={\int_0^1}F_{\alpha,X}(\sigma,\theta)
 F_{\beta,X}(\sigma,\theta)\e^{-2\pi i N\theta}\dd\theta.
\end{align*}
The second expression is a Hermitian tensor entry because, at real $\sigma$,
$\overline{(\mathcal CF)(\sigma,\theta)}=F(\sigma,\theta)$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact difference and sum selectors]\label{thm:marginals}
For every integer $h$ and $N$,
\begin{align*}
 \boxed{
 \mathcal D_{\alpha\beta,X}(h;\sigma)
 =\sum_{m-n=h}
 a_\alpha(m)\overline{a_\beta(n)}
 w_X(m)\overline{w_X(n)}(mn)^{-\sigma},}\\
 \boxed{
 \mathcal H_{\alpha\beta,X}(N;\sigma)
 =\sum_{m+n=N}
 a_\alpha(m)a_\beta(n)
 w_X(m)w_X(n)(mn)^{-\sigma}.}
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand both finite sums.  In the normal block the phase integral is
\[
 \int_0^1\e^{2\pi i(m-n-h)\theta}\dd\theta,
\]
which is one exactly when $m-n=h$ and zero otherwise.  In the anomalous block the corresponding phase is $m+n-N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Twin and Goldbach ports]\label{cor:twin-gold-port}
For the trivial character, $a_P(n)=\Lambda(n)$, and a window equal to one on the relevant range,
\[
 \mathcal D_{PP,X}(2;0)=\sum_{n\le X-2}\Lambda(n+2)\Lambda(n),
\]
while
\[
 \mathcal H_{PP,X}(N;0)=\sum_{m+n=N}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n).
\]
Twin primes and Goldbach are therefore different typed projections of one tensor: a normal difference block and an anomalous sum block.
\end{corollary}

\section{Source-to-future grand tensors and Schur shorting}

The rank-one tensor of \Cref{def:port-tensor} is the smallest kinematic case.  A physical or arithmetic history source generally has a Hilbert space $\mathcal S_X$, a finite port space $\mathcal P_X$, and a synthesis
\[
 S_X:\mathcal P_X\longrightarrow\mathcal S_X.
\]
Let $Q_X\succeq0$ be a preassigned future, reflection, or nuisance-short operator on $\mathcal S_X$.

\begin{definition}[Universal source-to-port grand tensor]\label{def:grand}
The grand tensor associated with $(S_X,Q_X)$ is
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb G_X=S_X^*Q_XS_X\succeq0.}
\]
Its entries retain all pairwise phases among the selected ports before any record-erasing diagonalization.
\end{definition}

Suppose the port space is split into signal and nuisance components and
\[
 \mathbb G_X=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A&B\\ B^*&C
 \end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Schur-short variational principle]\label{thm:schur}
Assume $\Ran B^*\subseteq\Ran C$.  The canonical positive short of $\mathbb G_X$ to the signal space is
\[
 \boxed{
 \Short_{\rm sig}(\mathbb G_X)=A-BC^\dagger B^*\succeq0,}
\]
where $C^\dagger$ is the Moore--Penrose inverse.  For every signal vector $x$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \ip{x}{\Short_{\rm sig}(\mathbb G_X)x}
 =\inf_y
 \ip{(x,y)}{\mathbb G_X(x,y)}.}
\]
The minimizer of least norm is $y=-C^\dagger B^*x$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand the quadratic form:
\[
 \ip{(x,y)}{\mathbb G_X(x,y)}
 =\ip{x}{Ax}+2\Re\ip{B^*x}{y}+\ip{y}{Cy}.
\]
Because $B^*x\in\Ran C$, write $B^*x=Cz$ with $z=C^\dagger B^*x$.  Then
\[
 \ip{y}{Cy}+2\Re\ip{Cz}{y}
 =\ip{y+z}{C(y+z)}-\ip{z}{Cz}.
\]
The infimum is attained at $y=-z$ modulo $\ker C$ and equals
\[
 \ip{x}{Ax}-\ip{C^\dagger B^*x}{B^*x}
 =\ip{x}{(A-BC^\dagger B^*)x}.
\]
Nonnegativity follows because the original quadratic form is nonnegative.
\end{proof}

\begin{warning}[Diagonal positivity is not quotient coercivity]
A lower bound for $A$, for $\Tr\mathbb G_X$, or for an unshorted route does not imply a lower bound for $A-BC^\dagger B^*$.  The nuisance can predict the entire signal.  Every conditional chain below therefore places the non-formal estimate on a connected or shortened object.
\end{warning}

\chapter{Mellin reflection and horizontal zero information}

\section{Finite reflection positivity}

Let $Z$ be a finite multiset invariant, with multiplicity, under
\[
 \mathcal R\rho=1-\bar\rho.
\]
For a polynomial $u$, define
\[
 \mathcal W_Z[u]
 =\sum_{\rho\in Z}u(\rho)\overline{u(\mathcal R\rho)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite reflected Weil criterion]\label{thm:finite-weil}
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item every point of $Z$ lies on $\Re s=1/2$;
\item $\mathcal W_Z[u]\ge0$ for every polynomial $u$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If every point is fixed by $\mathcal R$, then
\[
 \mathcal W_Z[u]=\sum_{\rho\in Z}|u(\rho)|^2\ge0.
\]
Conversely, if $\rho_0\ne\mathcal R\rho_0$, polynomial interpolation produces $u$ equal to $1$ at $\rho_0$, $-1$ at $\mathcal R\rho_0$, and $0$ at all other distinct points.  If the reflected pair has multiplicity $m$, its contribution is $-2m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ordinary frames do not locate zeros]
The energy $\sum_{\rho\in Z}|u(\rho)|^2$ is positive for every zero configuration.  Horizontal information enters only through the reflected cross-pairing.
\end{corollary}

\section{Infinite completed functions}

For a primitive Dirichlet character $\chi$, let $\Xi(s,\chi)$ denote a completed $L$-function normalized so that its nontrivial zeros are invariant under $s\mapsto1-\bar s$.  The standard Weil explicit formula identifies positivity of the infinite reflected form on an admissible test class with GRH for $L(s,\chi)$; the principal modulus-one specialization is RH.  We use this standard result as an external theorem rather than reproducing the full distributional explicit formula.  What will be proved here is the exact growth mechanism by which a Gaussian travelling port detects horizontal displacement.


\section{A Gaussian travelling port}

Fix $A>0$ and $\tau\in\R$, and put
\[
 K_{A,\tau}(s)=\exp\!\left(A\left(s-\frac12-i\tau\right)^2\right).
\]
The coefficient $K_{A,\tau}(\rho)$ is Gaussian in the ordinate of $\rho$ and is nonzero at every zero.  The standard completed explicit formula gives a prime-side travelling port of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-expansion}
 \mathcal G_{\chi,A,\tau}(r)
 =\mathcal R_{\chi,A,\tau}(r)
 -\sum_{\Xi(\rho,\chi)=0}
 m_\rho K_{A,\tau}(\rho)
 \e^{(\rho-1/2)r},
\end{equation}
where the sum converges locally uniformly, the coefficients are absolutely summable after grouping equal zeros, and $\mathcal R$ decays faster than every exponential as $r\to+\infty$.  This is the Gaussian specialization of the explicit formula; conductor, gamma, and trivial-zero terms are contained in $\mathcal R$.

Define
\[
 \Theta_\chi
 =\sup_{\Xi(\rho,\chi)=0}
 \left|\Re\rho-\frac12\right|.
\]
Reflection symmetry makes the rightmost displacement equal to $\Theta_\chi$.

\begin{lemma}[Laplace-pole obstruction]\label{lem:laplace-pole}
Let
\[
 f(r)=R(r)+\sum_jc_j\e^{\lambda_jr}
\]
on $[0,\infty)$, where $\sum_j|c_j|<\infty$, the real parts $\Re\lambda_j$ are bounded above, the sum converges locally uniformly, $R$ has an entire Laplace transform, and equal exponents have been grouped so that every $c_j\ne0$.  If
\[
 \int_0^\infty\e^{-2\sigma r}|f(r)|^2\dd r<\infty,
\]
then $\Re\lambda_j\le\sigma$ for every $j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\Re z>\sigma$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives absolute convergence of
\[
 \mathcal Lf(z)=\int_0^\infty f(r)\e^{-zr}\dd r.
\]
Hence $\mathcal Lf$ is holomorphic there.  On a right half-plane where termwise integration is initially valid,
\[
 \mathcal Lf(z)=\mathcal LR(z)+\sum_j\frac{c_j}{z-\lambda_j}.
\]
The right side gives a meromorphic continuation, with nonzero residue $c_j$ at every $\lambda_j$.  A pole with $\Re\lambda_j>\sigma$ contradicts holomorphy of the Laplace integral in that half-plane.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian Hardy criterion for GRH]\label{thm:gaussian-grh}
For fixed $\chi,A,\tau$, define
\[
 \mathscr E_{\chi,A,\tau}(\sigma)
 =\int_0^\infty\e^{-2\sigma r}
 |\mathcal G_{\chi,A,\tau}(r)|^2\dd r.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item if $\sigma>\Theta_\chi$, then $\mathscr E_{\chi,A,\tau}(\sigma)<\infty$;
\item if $\mathscr E_{\chi,A,\tau}(\sigma)<\infty$, then $\Theta_\chi\le\sigma$;
\item GRH for $L(s,\chi)$ is equivalent to finiteness of the energy for every $\sigma>0$.
\end{enumerate}
The modulus-one principal specialization is an equivalent RH criterion after the zeta-pole density is subtracted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\sigma>\Theta_\chi$, every exponential in \eqref{eq:zero-expansion} belongs to the weighted space.  Gaussian decay in the ordinates gives absolute summability, while $\mathcal R$ is harmless.  Conversely, apply \Cref{lem:laplace-pole} with $\lambda_\rho=\rho-1/2$.  Reflection symmetry turns the right-hand bound into the two-sided bound $|\Re\rho-1/2|\le\sigma$.  Finiteness for every positive $\sigma$ is therefore equivalent to $\Theta_\chi=0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Cutoff-matched prime-side energy}

Let
\[
 G_A(v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi A}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{v^2}{4A}\right).
\]
For a Dirichlet character $\chi$, define the cutoff-matched arithmetic measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff-measure}
 \dd\mathfrak e_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}(u)
 =\sum_{n\le X}\chi(n)\Lambda(n)\delta_{\log n}(\dd u)
 -\delta_\chi\1_{[0,\log X]}(u)\e^u\dd u,
\end{equation}
where $\delta_\chi=1$ in the principal channel and $0$ otherwise.  Define
\[
 \mathfrak L_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}(s)
 =\int\e^{-su}\dd\mathfrak e_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}(u)
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-gaussian-port}
 \mathcal G_{\chi,A,\tau;X}^{\triangle}(r)
 =\e^{i\tau r}
 \int\e^{-(1/2+i\tau)u}
 G_A(u-r)\dd\mathfrak e_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}(u).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Gaussian Plancherel identity]\label{thm:plancherel}
For every real $\sigma$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 &\int_{\R}\e^{-2\sigma r}
 \left|\mathcal G_{\chi,A,\tau;X}^{\triangle}(r)\right|^2\dd r\\
 &\quad=\frac{\e^{2A\sigma^2}}{2\pi}
 \int_{\R}\e^{-2A(t-\tau)^2}
 \left|\mathfrak L_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}
 \left(\frac12+\sigma+it\right)\right|^2\dd t.
 \end{aligned}}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the Fourier convention $\widehat f(t)=\int f(r)\e^{-itr}\dd r$, for which Plancherel has factor $(2\pi)^{-1}$.  The Fourier transform of $G_A$ is $\e^{-At^2}$.  For the weighted function $\e^{-\sigma r}\mathcal G(r)$, a change of variable $r=u+v$ gives
\[
 \widehat{\e^{-\sigma\cdot}\mathcal G}(t)
 =\exp\!\left(A(-\sigma+i(\tau-t))^2\right)
 \mathfrak L_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}
 \left(\frac12+\sigma+it\right).
\]
Taking the modulus squared and applying Plancherel proves the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite scaling indicator]\label{thm:scaling-indicator}
Let
\[
 \mathscr E_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}(\sigma)
 =\int_{\R}\e^{-2\sigma r}
 |\mathcal G_{\chi,A,\tau;X}^{\triangle}(r)|^2\dd r.
\]
The standard explicit formula and its cutoff-matched bulk approximation imply
\[
 \boxed{
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}
 \frac{\log^+\mathscr E_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}(\sigma)}{\log X}
 =2(\Theta_\chi-\sigma)_+.}
\]
Consequently, the estimate
\[
 \mathscr E_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}(\sigma)=X^{o(1)}
\]
forces $\Theta_\chi\le\sigma$; imposing it for every $\sigma>0$ gives GRH for $\chi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We include the standard argument because it identifies exactly what must be estimated.  Put $U=\log X$.  The cutoff-matched port agrees with the complete explicit-formula port, with super-Gaussian error, on every bulk interval $r\le(1-\delta)U$.  This follows directly from the Gaussian tail because both the atomic and continuous measures are cut at the same endpoint.

For the upper bound, the explicit formula gives, for every $\eps>0$,
\[
 |\mathcal G_{\chi,A,\tau}(r)|
 \ll_{\eps,A,\chi}(1+r)^C
 \e^{(\Theta_\chi+\eps)r}
\]
for $r\ge0$.  Integrating to $U$ and controlling the moving edge by the same Gaussian estimate gives
\[
 \mathscr E_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}(\sigma)
 \ll X^{2(\Theta_\chi-\sigma+\eps)_+}(\log X)^C.
\]

For the lower bound, choose a zero with displacement
\[
 \Re\rho-\frac12>\Theta_\chi-\eps.
\]
Group zero terms with the same real part and use the Bohr mean-square identity for the resulting absolutely convergent almost-periodic exponential sum: its mean square is the sum of the squared grouped coefficients and is positive because the Gaussian coefficient of the chosen zero is nonzero.  Hence on arbitrarily long intervals the complete weighted energy is bounded below by a positive multiple of
\[
 \exp\!\left(2(\Theta_\chi-\sigma-2\eps)r\right).
\]
Integrating inside $[0,(1-\delta)U]$, where the finite and complete ports agree, yields
\[
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}
 \frac{\log^+\mathscr E_{\chi,X}^{\triangle}(\sigma)}{\log X}
 \ge2(1-\delta)(\Theta_\chi-\sigma-2\eps)_+.
\]
Let $\delta,\eps\downarrow0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Fixed-modulus vector form}

Let $q$ be prime, let $\mathcal H_\sigma$ be the weighted Gaussian port space, and let $v_a\in\mathcal H_\sigma$ be the prime packet in the reduced residue class $a\bmod q$.  Put
\[
 \bar v=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{a\in(\Z/q\Z)^\times}v_a.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Character--residue Parseval identity]\label{thm:residue-parseval}
With
\[
 G_\chi=\sum_a\chi(a)v_a,
\]
one has
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_a\|v_a-\bar v\|^2
 =\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\ne\chi_0}\|G_\chi\|^2.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite characters form an orthogonal basis of functions on the unit group.  Parseval gives
\[
 \sum_a\|v_a\|^2
 =\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_\chi\|G_\chi\|^2.
\]
The principal term is
\[
 \frac{1}{q-1}\|G_{\chi_0}\|^2=(q-1)\|\bar v\|^2.
\]
Subtract it.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional fixed-modulus GRH chain]\label{cor:fixed-mod-grh}
Fix $q$ and $\sigma>0$.  If the cutoff-matched residue variance satisfies
\[
 \sum_a\|v_{a;X}-\bar v_X\|_{\mathcal H_\sigma}^2=X^{o(1)},
\]
then every nonprincipal zero modulo $q$ obeys
\[
 \left|\Re\rho-\frac12\right|\le\sigma.
\]
If this estimate holds for every $\sigma>0$, all nonprincipal Dirichlet $L$-functions modulo $q$ satisfy GRH.  Adding the centered principal channel gives RH as well.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{thm:residue-parseval}, every nonprincipal character energy is bounded by the total residue variance.  Apply \Cref{thm:scaling-indicator} character by character.  The principal channel uses the density subtraction in \eqref{eq:cutoff-measure}.
\end{proof}

\chapter{Typed Dirichlet channels and compact Mellin packets}

\section{Primitive typing and the functional equation}

Let $\chi$ be a primitive Dirichlet character of conductor $q$, extended by
zero away from the units, and put
\[
 a_\chi=\frac{1-\chi(-1)}2\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The completed function is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v002-completed-L}
 \Lambda(s,\chi)
 =\left(\frac q\pi\right)^{(s+a_\chi)/2}
 \Gamma\!\left(\frac{s+a_\chi}{2}\right)L(s,\chi).
\end{equation}
For primitive nonprincipal $\chi$ it is entire; the primitive principal
channel is the modulus-one zeta channel and is meromorphic at $0$ and $1$.

\begin{theorem}[Typed primitive functional equation]\label{thm:v002-functional-equation}
For primitive $\chi$ there is a root number $\varepsilon_\chi$ with
$|\varepsilon_\chi|=1$ such that
\[
 \boxed{\Lambda(s,\chi)=\varepsilon_\chi
 \Lambda(1-s,\overline\chi).}
\]
Moreover $\varepsilon_{\overline\chi}=\overline{\varepsilon_\chi}$, and,
where both sides are defined and nonzero,
\[
 \boxed{\frac{\Lambda'}{\Lambda}(s,\chi)
 =-\frac{\Lambda'}{\Lambda}(1-s,\overline\chi).}
\]
For a complex character the type-correct carrier is the doubled channel
\[
 \boldsymbol\Lambda_\chi(s)
 =\binom{\Lambda(s,\chi)}{\Lambda(s,\overline\chi)},
 \qquad
 \mathsf R_\chi=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&\varepsilon_\chi\\
 \overline{\varepsilon_\chi}&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
for which $\mathsf R_\chi$ is a self-adjoint unitary and
\[
 \boxed{\boldsymbol\Lambda_\chi(s)
 =\mathsf R_\chi\boldsymbol\Lambda_\chi(1-s).}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The scalar identity is the standard primitive Dirichlet functional equation
in the normalization \eqref{eq:v002-completed-L}.  The Gauss-sum identity
$\tau(\overline\chi)=\chi(-1)\overline{\tau(\chi)}$ gives the stated
conjugation law for the root number.  Logarithmic differentiation removes
the constant root number and contributes the minus sign from $1-s$.
Applying the scalar identity to $\chi$ and $\overline\chi$ gives the matrix
identity.  The root-number relation and $|\varepsilon_\chi|=1$ imply
$\mathsf R_\chi^*=\mathsf R_\chi$ and $\mathsf R_\chi^2=I$.
\end{proof}

\begin{warning}[Complex-character typing]
For complex $\chi$, the functional equation exchanges the $\chi$ and
$\overline\chi$ sectors.  Replacing them by one real history space without a
specified isometric or antiunitary intertwiner can erase the reflected phase
which the grand tensor is designed to retain.
\end{warning}

\section{Imprimitive reduction}

Let a character $\chi\pmod Q$ be induced by a primitive character
$\chi^*\pmod q$, where $q\mid Q$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact imprimitive Euler correction]\label{prop:v002-imprimitive}
One has
\[
 \boxed{L(s,\chi)=L(s,\chi^*)
 \prod_{\substack{p\mid Q\\p\nmid q}}
 \left(1-\chi^*(p)p^{-s}\right),}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{-\frac{L'}{L}(s,\chi)
 =-\frac{L'}{L}(s,\chi^*)
 -\sum_{\substack{p\mid Q\\p\nmid q}}
 \frac{\chi^*(p)(\log p)p^{-s}}
 {1-\chi^*(p)p^{-s}}.}
\]
Every zero of an added Euler factor lies on $\Re s=0$.  Hence the zeros of
$L(s,\chi)$ in $0<\Re s<1$ are precisely the zeros of $L(s,\chi^*)$, with
multiplicity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Compare the two Euler products.  At primes not dividing $Q$ they agree.  At
$p\mid Q$ with $p\nmid q$, the imprimitive character vanishes while the
primitive Euler factor remains, giving the finite product.  Logarithmic
differentiation gives the second identity.  If
$1-\chi^*(p)p^{-s}=0$, then $|p^{-s}|=1$, so $p^{-\Re s}=1$ and therefore
$\Re s=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Primitive reduction of Dirichlet GRH]
It is enough to prove the critical-line assertion separately for every
primitive Dirichlet $L$-function.  The reduction itself needs no uniformity
in the conductor.
\end{corollary}

\section{A compact logarithmic packet}

Fix $C\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ and define its bilateral Laplace transform
\[
 H_C(s)=\int_\R C(u)e^{su}\,\dd u.
\]
For $X>0$ put
\[
 \mathcal P_{C,\chi}(X)
 =\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{\sqrt n}
 C\!\left(\log\frac nX\right),
\]
and
\[
 \mathcal C_C(X)=\int_1^\infty y^{-1/2}
 C\!\left(\log\frac yX\right)\,\dd y.
\]
Let $\delta_\chi=1$ in the primitive trivial channel and $0$ otherwise, and
set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v002-centered-packet}
 \boxed{\mathcal E_{C,\chi}(X)
 =\mathcal P_{C,\chi}(X)-\delta_\chi\mathcal C_C(X).}
\end{equation}
Both terms vanish for all sufficiently small $X$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact twisted Mellin transform]\label{thm:v002-twisted-mellin}
For $\Re s>1/2$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^\infty \mathcal E_{C,\chi}(X)X^{-s}\frac{\dd X}{X}
 =H_C(s)\left[
 -\frac{L'}{L}\!\left(s+\frac12,\chi\right)
 -\frac{\delta_\chi}{s-1/2}\right].}
\]
The integral is absolutely convergent in that half-plane.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $n$, the substitution $u=\log(n/X)$ gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty C\!\left(\log\frac nX\right)
 X^{-s}\frac{\dd X}{X}=n^{-s}H_C(s).
\]
Absolute convergence for $\Re s>1/2$ permits Fubini and the Dirichlet series
for $-L'/L$.  The same substitution with $n$ replaced by $y$ gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty\mathcal C_C(X)X^{-s}\frac{\dd X}{X}
 =H_C(s)\int_1^\infty y^{-s-1/2}\dd y
 =\frac{H_C(s)}{s-1/2}.
\]
Subtracting proves the formula.
\end{proof}

\section{Conductor, Gamma factor, and trivial-zero cancellation}

Put
\[
 \mathcal G_\chi(w)
 =\frac12\log\frac q\pi
 +\frac12\psi\!\left(\frac{w+a_\chi}{2}\right),
 \qquad \psi=\Gamma'/\Gamma.
\]
Then
\[
 \frac{\Lambda'}{\Lambda}(w,\chi)
 =\mathcal G_\chi(w)+\frac{L'}{L}(w,\chi).
\]
Define the archimedean packet by inverse Mellin transform on any line
$\Re s=\sigma>1/2$,
\[
 \mathcal A_{C,\chi}^{(\sigma)}(X)
 =-\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}
 H_C(s)\mathcal G_\chi(s+1/2)X^s\,\dd s,
\]
and set $\mathcal E_{C,\chi}^{\Lambda}
=\mathcal E_{C,\chi}+\mathcal A_{C,\chi}^{(\sigma)}$.

\begin{proposition}[Completed packet symbol and trivial zeros]
\label{prop:v002-completed-packet}
The completed packet has Mellin symbol
\[
 \boxed{
 \widehat{\mathcal E}_{C,\chi}^{\Lambda}(s)
 =H_C(s)\left[-\frac{\Lambda'}{\Lambda}
 \!\left(s+\frac12,\chi\right)
 -\frac{\delta_\chi}{s-1/2}\right].}
\]
For primitive nonprincipal $\chi$, the poles of $L'/L$ at the parity-forced
trivial zeros $w=-a_\chi-2m$ cancel exactly against the corresponding poles
of the digamma term.  Consequently only nontrivial zeros contribute poles in
the open critical strip.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The inverse Mellin definition contributes the symbol
$-H_C(s)\mathcal G_\chi(s+1/2)$; add it to
\Cref{thm:v002-twisted-mellin}.  Near $w_m=-a_\chi-2m$,
\[
 \frac12\psi\!\left(\frac{w+a_\chi}{2}\right)
 =-\frac1{w-w_m}+O(1),
 \qquad
 \frac{L'}{L}(w,\chi)=\frac1{w-w_m}+O(1),
\]
so the principal parts cancel.
\end{proof}

\section{Off-line zeros force power growth}

\begin{lemma}[Packet selection]
For every $s_0=\alpha+i\gamma$ with $\alpha>0$, there exists a fixed
$C\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ with $H_C(s_0)\ne0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose nonzero $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ with $\phi\ge0$ and put
$C(u)=e^{-i\gamma u}\phi(u)$.  Then
$H_C(s_0)=\int\phi(u)e^{\alpha u}\dd u>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Off-critical zero amplification]\label{thm:v002-zero-amplification}
Suppose $L(\rho,\chi)=0$ for primitive $\chi$, with
$\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ and $\beta>1/2$.  Put $\alpha=\beta-1/2$ and choose
$C$ with $H_C(\rho-1/2)\ne0$.  Then, for every $\eps>0$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}
 \frac{|\mathcal E_{C,\chi}(X)|}{X^{\alpha-\eps}}=\infty.}
\]
The same assertion holds for the completed packet
$\mathcal E_{C,\chi}^{\Lambda}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The meromorphic continuation of the Mellin transform in
\Cref{thm:v002-twisted-mellin} has a pole at $s_0=\rho-1/2$ with residue
$-m_\rho H_C(s_0)\ne0$.  If
$\mathcal E_{C,\chi}(X)=O(X^{\alpha-\eps})$, then, because the packet
vanishes for sufficiently small $X$, its Mellin integral would converge
absolutely and be holomorphic in $\Re s>\alpha-\eps$.  On the intersection
with $\Re s>1/2$ it equals the meromorphic expression above, so the identity
theorem would make that expression holomorphic at $s_0$, a contradiction.
The completed packet has the same nontrivial-zero residue because the
conductor--Gamma term is holomorphic at $\rho$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Packetwise subpower property]
A primitive character $\chi$ satisfies $\mathrm{SP}(\chi)$ if, for every
$C\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ and every $\eta>0$,
\[
 \mathcal E_{C,\chi}(X)=O_{C,\chi,\eta}(X^\eta).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Packetwise conditional GRH]\label{thm:v002-packet-grh}
If $\mathrm{SP}(\chi)$ holds for a primitive $\chi$, then
$L(s,\chi)\ne0$ for $\Re s>1/2$.  If it holds for every primitive character,
then Dirichlet GRH holds for all primitive and imprimitive Dirichlet
$L$-functions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
An off-line zero with displacement $\alpha>0$ contradicts
\Cref{thm:v002-zero-amplification} and the subpower estimate with any
$\eta<\alpha/2$.  Thus the right half of the strip is zero-free.  The typed
functional equation reflects any zero left of the critical line into the
right half-plane of the conjugate primitive channel.  Finally apply
\Cref{prop:v002-imprimitive}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is a character-by-character reduction.  No conductor-uniform estimate
is needed logically.  Uniformity becomes relevant only for a stronger
family theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite frames are phase references, not zero selectors}

\begin{proposition}[Finite self-translation no-go]\label{prop:v002-translation-nogo}
Let $Z=\{z_1,\ldots,z_M\}$ be a finite multiset and let
$\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_k$ be such that all $z_i-\eta_j$ are distinct from $Z$
and from one another.  Given arbitrary nonzero values $A_{ij}$, there is an
entire function $F$ whose zero multiset contains $Z$ and for which
\[
 F(z_i-\eta_j)=A_{ij}.
\]
Therefore a finite lower frame of horizontally translated values does not
constrain the horizontal positions of the zeros.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $P(z)$ be the polynomial with zero multiset $Z$.  At every shifted sample
point prescribe
\[
 h(z_i-\eta_j)=\log\!\left(\frac{A_{ij}}{P(z_i-\eta_j)}\right),
\]
choosing arbitrary logarithm branches.  Polynomial interpolation gives a
polynomial $h$ with these values.  Then $F=Pe^h$ has the desired zeros and
samples.
\end{proof}

\begin{audit}[What the Dirichlet collection adds]
The compact packet theorem and the cutoff-matched Gaussian theorem already
present in the monograph are complementary.  The former gives a minimal
Mellin-pole contradiction for one fixed compact packet; the latter gives a
positive Hilbert-space energy with an exact horizontal-displacement exponent.
Neither auxiliary-character lower frames nor finite self-translation frames
supply the missing reflected prime-side sign by themselves.
\end{audit}

\chapter{Prolate compression and a separate conditional RH route}

\section{The time--band limiting operator}

Let $P_T$ be restriction to $[-T,T]$ on $L^2(\R)$ and let
$B_\Omega=\mathcal F^{-1}\1_{[-\Omega,\Omega]}\mathcal F$ be the orthogonal
band-limiting projection, with the unitary Fourier transform.  On
$L^2([-T,T])$ define
\[
 K_{T,\Omega}=P_TB_\Omega P_T.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Basic prolate concentration theorem]\label{thm:v002-prolate-basic}
The operator $K_{T,\Omega}$ is compact, positive, self-adjoint, and satisfies
$0\preceq K_{T,\Omega}\preceq I$.  Its integral kernel is
\[
 K_{T,\Omega}(x,y)=\frac{\sin(\Omega(x-y))}{\pi(x-y)},
\]
with diagonal value $\Omega/\pi$, and
\[
 \boxed{\Tr K_{T,\Omega}=\frac{2T\Omega}{\pi}.}
\]
If $1>\lambda_0\ge\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge0$ are its eigenvalues, then for
every rank-$m$ orthogonal projection $P$,
\[
 \Tr(PK_{T,\Omega}P)\le\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\lambda_j.
\]
Thus the first $m$ prolate eigenfunctions give the optimal $m$-dimensional
subspace for simultaneous time and frequency concentration.  This is the
classical prolate time--band limiting operator of
\cite{SlepianPollak,LandauPollak}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The displayed kernel follows by Fourier inversion of the band projection.
On the bounded square $[-T,T]^2$ it is square integrable, so the operator is
Hilbert--Schmidt and hence compact.  Positivity and the contraction bound
follow from
\[
 \langle f,K_{T,\Omega}f\rangle
 =\|B_\Omega P_Tf\|_2^2\in[0,\|f\|_2^2].
\]
The continuous positive kernel is trace class, and integrating the diagonal
gives $2T\Omega/\pi$.  The final assertion is the Ky Fan maximum principle.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact radical-to-window leakage}

Let $\mathcal H$ carry a Hermitian form $QW(\cdot,\cdot)$ on a common form
domain.  Let $F$ be in its radical, so $QW(F,h)=0$ for all admissible $h$.
Let $P_{\rm win}$ be a window projection and $P_N$ a finite Galerkin
projection.  Write
\[
 g=P_{\rm win}F,\qquad r=(I-P_{\rm win})F,
 \qquad \phi=P_Ng,\qquad s=(I-P_N)g.
\]
Let $Q_N$ be the Galerkin operator representing $QW$ on $\Ran P_N$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact leakage identity]\label{thm:v002-leakage}
For every $h\in\Ran P_N$,
\[
 \boxed{\langle h,Q_N\phi\rangle=-QW(r,h)-QW(s,h).}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \|Q_N\phi\|
 \le
 \sup_{\substack{h\in\Ran P_N\\\|h\|=1}}
 |QW(r,h)+QW(s,h)|.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $F=\phi+s+r$ and $F$ belongs to the radical,
\[
 0=QW(F,h)=QW(\phi,h)+QW(s,h)+QW(r,h).
\]
For $\phi,h\in\Ran P_N$, the first term is
$\langle h,Q_N\phi\rangle$.  Taking the supremum gives the norm bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This identity is the exact advantage of the prolate route: the residual of a
specified near-radical packet is entirely the image of its window and
Galerkin leakage.  Ordinary $L^2$ concentration is not automatically enough;
the leakage must be small in the graph norm in which the Weil form is
continuous.
\end{remark}

\section{Cluster--Feshbach reduction}

\begin{theorem}[Separated cluster and Feshbach short]\label{thm:v002-cluster}
Let $Q=Q^*$ act on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and let $\Pi$ be an
orthogonal projection.  Write
\[
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}A&B^*\\B&C\end{pmatrix}
\]
relative to $\Ran\Pi\oplus\Ran(I-\Pi)$.  Put
\[
 a_+=\max\Spec A,\qquad c_-=\min\Spec C,
 \qquad\Delta=c_--a_+>0,\qquad\beta=\|B\|.
\]
If $\beta<\Delta/2$, then the spectral projection $P$ of $Q$ below
$(a_++c_-)/2$ has rank $\rank\Pi$ and
\[
 \boxed{\|P-\Pi\|\le\frac{2\beta}{\Delta-2\beta}.}
\]
For every real $z<c_-$,
\[
 \boxed{\det(Q-z)=\det(C-z)
 \det\!\left(A-z-B^*(C-z)^{-1}B\right),}
\]
and
\[
 0\preceq B^*(C-z)^{-1}B
 \preceq\frac{\beta^2}{c_--z}I.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $Q_0=A\oplus C$.  The off-diagonal perturbation $Q-Q_0$ has norm
$\beta$.  Since the two spectral sets of $Q_0$ are separated by $\Delta$ and
$\beta<\Delta/2$, no eigenvalue crosses the midpoint; the low spectral
projection has the same rank.  The resolvent representation of spectral
projections on a contour through the gap, followed by the resolvent identity,
gives the displayed safe sin-$\Theta$ bound.  For $z<c_-$, Gaussian block
elimination gives the determinant identity.  Positivity and the norm bound
for $(C-z)^{-1}$ give the final operator inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Simple-even extraction]
Assume the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:v002-cluster}.  If $A$ has a simple lowest
eigenvalue $a_0$ with internal gap $\gamma>0$, and
$2\beta^2/\Delta<\gamma$, then the lowest eigenvalue of $Q$ is simple and its
compressed eigenvector differs from that of $A$ by
$O(\beta^2/(\Delta\gamma))$.  If an involution commuting with $Q$ and $\Pi$
makes the ground vector of $A$ even, and the odd compressed spectrum begins
more than $\beta^2/\Delta$ above $a_0$, then the ground vector of $Q$ is even.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For eigenvalues below $c_-$, the effective operator is
$A-B^*(C-z)^{-1}B$, whose correction has norm at most $\beta^2/\Delta$ at
the lower edge.  The min--max principle gives simplicity, and the standard
simple-eigenvalue perturbation estimate gives the angle bound.  The same
self-energy can lower the odd sector by no more than its norm, proving the
parity statement.
\end{proof}

\section{Conditional PSWF--Hurwitz chain}

\begin{theorem}[Real-rooted approximation implies RH]\label{thm:v002-hurwitz-rh}
Let $D_n$ be nonzero entire functions whose zeros are all real.  Suppose
$D_n\to\Xi$ locally uniformly on $\C$, where $\Xi$ is the completed Riemann
xi function and is not identically zero.  Then all zeros of $\Xi$ are real;
equivalently RH holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\Xi$ had a nonreal zero $z_0$, choose a closed disk around $z_0$ which is
disjoint from the real axis and whose boundary contains no zero of $\Xi$.
Every $D_n$ is zero-free on that disk.  Hurwitz's theorem says that a locally
uniform limit of zero-free holomorphic functions is either zero-free or
identically zero there.  This contradicts the zero of $\Xi$ and the fact
that $\Xi$ is not identically zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional prolate cluster route to RH]\label{thm:v002-pswf-chain}
Assume a sequence of finite self-adjoint Weil matrices $Q_n$ and physically
preassigned prolate projections $\Pi_n$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\|B_n\|/\Delta_n\to0$ in the cluster decomposition of
      \Cref{thm:v002-cluster};
\item the compressed matrix $A_n=\Pi_nQ_n\Pi_n$ has a simple even ground
      vector $k_n$, with internal and parity gaps dominating
      $\|B_n\|^2/\Delta_n$;
\item the leakage identity \Cref{thm:v002-leakage} gives convergence of the
      actual ground vector to $k_n$ in a norm strong enough that the associated
      regularized determinants $D_n$ converge locally uniformly to $\Xi$;
\item each $D_n$ has only real zeros.
\end{enumerate}
Then RH holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The cluster and simple-even estimates identify the actual finite ground
vector with the preassigned prolate model.  Hypothesis (iii) transfers this
to locally uniform determinant convergence.  Apply
\Cref{thm:v002-hurwitz-rh}.
\end{proof}

\begin{audit}[What the PSWF collection contributes]
The prolate route is not another form of full Weil positivity.  It replaces
the full test space by a growing near-radical cluster and separates two new
estimates: cluster-to-exterior leakage and the arithmetic ordering inside the
compressed cluster.  Neither time--frequency concentration nor the Feshbach
identity supplies the internal sign automatically.
\end{audit}

\chapter{Affine-invariant PNT crests and the scalar-bulk firewall}

\section{A two-channel Weyl inequality}

Let $L=L^*$ and $D=D^*$ on an $n$-dimensional Hilbert space, $n\ge2$.  Write
$\ell_1\le\ell_2\le\cdots$ for the eigenvalues of $L$ and
$d_1\ge d_2\ge\cdots$ for the eigenvalues of $D$ in decreasing order.  For a
unit vector $g$, put
\[
 m=\langle g,Lg\rangle,\qquad \bar d=\langle g,Dg\rangle,
 \qquad \mu=m-\bar d,
\]
and
\[
 \Gamma^{\rm W}(L,D;g)
 =\max\{(\ell_2-m)-(d_1-\bar d),
          (\ell_1-m)-(d_2-\bar d)\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Scalar-invariant two-channel Weyl barrier]
\label{thm:v002-weyl-barrier}
One has
\[
 \boxed{\lambda_2(L-D)-\mu\ge\Gamma^{\rm W}(L,D;g).}
\]
The right side is invariant under $L\mapsto L+bI$ and
$D\mapsto D+aI$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two Weyl inequalities
$\lambda_2(L-D)\ge\ell_2-d_1$ and
$\lambda_2(L-D)\ge\ell_1-d_2$ give the two terms after subtracting
$\mu=m-\bar d$.  Scalar shifts cancel from eigenvalue--Rayleigh differences.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The second channel matters when one exceptional positive eigen-direction of
$D$ destroys the first bound.  It is the finite-dimensional statement that
one large PNT crest can lower at most one spectral level.
\end{remark}

\section{A precise scalar-bulk no-go theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Scalar bulk carries no ordering]\label{thm:v002-scalar-bulk}
Let $P$ be an orthogonal projection of rank at least two, let $g\in\Ran P$ be
a unit vector, and assume
\[
 \|P(L-cI-D)P\|\le\varepsilon.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\Gamma^{\rm W}(L,D;g)\le2\varepsilon.}
\]
In particular, if $\varepsilon=o(a)$ on a growing physical subspace, no
leading scalar term $cI$ can provide a gap of order $a$ through the affine
Weyl barrier.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By min--max on the rank-at-least-two trial space $\Ran P$,
\[
 \ell_2\le c+d_1+\varepsilon.
\]
Also the minimum Rayleigh quotient of $D$ on any two-dimensional subspace is
at most $d_2$, so
\[
 \ell_1\le c+d_2+\varepsilon.
\]
Because $g\in\Ran P$,
$|m-c-\bar d|\le\varepsilon$.  Substitution into both channels gives an
upper bound $2\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

\begin{warning}[Critical-scale firewall]
A positive generator may be asymptotic to a scalar on a large PSWF family.
Positivity, Perron simplicity, or leading-order oscillation of that scalar
bulk then supplies no ordering of the first two arithmetic levels.  A valid
continuation must sign a next-scale centered matrix: a compressed PNT
crest, a conductor recursion coefficient, or a renormalized
cluster--complement Schur return.
\end{warning}

\part{Additive prime chains}

\chapter{Exact prime and factor-depth selectors}

\section{Higher prime powers are lower order}

\begin{lemma}[Prime-power contamination]\label{lem:prime-power}
For fixed $h$, the contribution to
\[
 \sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+h)
\]
from terms for which at least one coordinate is a proper prime power is
\[
 O_h(X^{1/2}\log X).
\]
The analogous contribution to $\sum_{m+n=N}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)$ is
$O(N^{1/2}\log N)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The total von Mangoldt weight of proper prime powers up to $Y$ is
\[
 \sum_{k\ge2}\sum_{p\le Y^{1/k}}\log p
 \ll \sqrt Y.
\]
For every exceptional coordinate the other factor is at most $O(\log Y)$, and there are two choices of exceptional coordinate.
\end{proof}

\section{Ordered-factor CAR projector}

Let $n=p_1\cdots p_k$ be squarefree and let $\mathcal O_n=\ell^2(\mathfrak S_k)$ be its ordered factor-history space.  Let $c_p^*$ be fermionic creation operators and define
\[
 E_n|\pi\rangle
 =c_{p_{\pi(1)}}^*\cdots c_{p_{\pi(k)}}^*|0\rangle.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact ordered-history prime filter]\label{thm:car-prime}
For every $\pi\in\mathfrak S_k$,
\[
 E_n|\pi\rangle=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)|n\rangle.
\]
Let
\[
 |\Omega_k^+\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt{k!}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_k}|\pi\rangle.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 E_n|\Omega_k^+\rangle
 =\begin{cases}
 |n\rangle,&k=1,\\
 0,&k\ge2.
 \end{cases}}
\]
Thus the permutation-symmetric ordered-history source followed by the CAR endpoint is an exact prime projector on squarefree targets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Anticommutation changes the ordered product by the sign of the permutation, proving the first formula.  Hence
\[
 E_n|\Omega_k^+\rangle
 =\frac{1}{\sqrt{k!}}
 \left(\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_k}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\right)|n\rangle.
\]
For $k=1$ the sum is one; for $k\ge2$ the even and odd permutations pair and cancel.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This theorem solves the algebraic separation problem but not the arithmetic support problem.  It identifies the prime branch if a source reaches it; it does not prove that the branch has nonzero mass.
\end{remark}

\section{The parity-current identity on a \texorpdfstring{$P_1\cup P_2$}{P1 union P2} carrier}

Let a positive weighted candidate carrier be known to contain only squarefree targets of factor depth one or two.  Write
\[
 M=P_1+P_2
\]
for its total mass and
\[
 \mathcal J=\sum_cw_c\mu(n_c)
\]
for its M\"obius grading current.  Since $\mu=-1$ on primes and $+1$ on squarefree semiprimes,
\[
 \mathcal J=-P_1+P_2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact depth-one recovery]\label{thm:parity-recovery}
On a positive $P_1\cup P_2$ carrier,
\[
 \boxed{
 P_1=\frac12(M-\mathcal J),
 \qquad
 P_2=\frac12(M+\mathcal J).}
\]
If $M>0$ and $\mathcal J=o(M)$, then
\[
 P_1=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)M>0.
\]
More generally, $\mathcal J\le(1-2\delta)M$ implies $P_1\ge\delta M$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Solve the two linear equations $M=P_1+P_2$ and $\mathcal J=-P_1+P_2$.
\end{proof}

\chapter{Conditional twin-prime and Goldbach theorems}

\section{Twin primes from a rough positive carrier}

Let $b_{p,X}\ge0$ be weights supported on source primes $p$ in a dyadic interval, and suppose the selected target $p+2$ is known to be either prime or squarefree semiprime.  Define
\[
 M_X=\sum_pb_{p,X},
 \qquad
 \mathcal J_X=\sum_pb_{p,X}\mu(p+2).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Conditional twin-prime chain]\label{thm:twin-chain}
Assume that along an unbounded sequence $X\to\infty$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(T\arabic*)}]
\item $b_{p,X}\ge0$ and every supported target $p+2$ lies in $P_1\cup P_2$;
\item $M_X\ge m_0>0$ after the chosen normalization;
\item $\mathcal J_X=o(M_X)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then there is a supported twin-prime pair for every sufficiently large scale in the sequence, and hence infinitely many twin primes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{thm:parity-recovery}, the weighted prime-target mass is
\[
 P_{1,X}=\frac12(M_X-\mathcal J_X)
 =\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)M_X>0.
\]
A positive sum of nonnegative weights over prime targets implies that at least one supported $p$ has $p+2$ prime.  Since the source intervals tend to infinity, the pairs are distinct and unbounded.
\end{proof}

\section{Binary Goldbach from a positive fixed-sum carrier}

For an even integer $N$, let $w_{p,N}\ge0$ be weights on source primes $p<N$ such that $N-p$ is known to lie in $P_1\cup P_2$.  Put
\[
 M_N=\sum_pw_{p,N},
 \qquad
 \mathcal J_N=\sum_pw_{p,N}\mu(N-p).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Conditional binary Goldbach chain]\label{thm:gold-chain}
Assume for every sufficiently large even $N$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(G\arabic*)}]
\item the weights are nonnegative and every target $N-p$ lies in $P_1\cup P_2$;
\item $M_N\ge m_0>0$ after normalization;
\item $\mathcal J_N=o(M_N)$ uniformly in $N$.
\end{enumerate}
Then every sufficiently large even $N$ is a sum of two primes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The prime-target mass equals
\[
 P_{1,N}=\frac12(M_N-\mathcal J_N)>0.
\]
Hence some supported source prime $p$ has $N-p$ prime.
\end{proof}

\section{Hardy--Littlewood asymptotic route}

\begin{theorem}[Classical asymptotic sufficient conditions]\label{thm:HL-sufficient}
Suppose, for some $\delta<1/2$,
\[
 \sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n)\Lambda(n+2)
 =\Sing(2)X+O(X^{1/2+\delta}),
 \qquad \Sing(2)>0,
\]
and, uniformly for sufficiently large even $N$,
\[
 \sum_{m+n=N}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)
 =\Sing(N)N+O(N^{1/2+\delta}),
 \qquad \inf_{N\ \mathrm{even}}\Sing(N)>0.
\]
Then there are infinitely many twin primes and every sufficiently large even integer is a sum of two primes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The main terms dominate the errors.  By \Cref{lem:prime-power}, contributions involving proper prime powers are $o(X)$ and $o(N)$.  The remaining positive von Mangoldt mass is carried by prime--prime pairs.
\end{proof}

\begin{warning}[Parity firewall]
The exact Fourier selectors and a positive frame do not prove the sign estimate in \Cref{thm:twin-chain,thm:gold-chain}.  A prime-free semiprime carrier can satisfy all local congruence conditions and carry large positive tensor energy.  The substantive input is a selector-stable, phase-sensitive current estimate or an equivalent marked zero-mode lower bound.
\end{warning}

\part{Degree-resolved Goldbach and twin-prime machinery}

\chapter{Squarefree Mellin degree and exact prime separation}

\section{The squarefree Mellin product}

Fix $X\ge2$ and put
\[
 L_X:=\log(2X).
\]
For $n\ge2$ define the entire squarefree Mellin port
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak E_{z,X}(n)
 :=\mu^2(n)\prod_{\ell\mid n}
       \left(\ell^{z/L_X}-1\right)
 =\sum_{j\ge1}B_{j,X}(n)z^j.
 \label{eq:v003-squarefree-mellin}
\end{equation}
The product is over the distinct prime divisors of $n$.  The factor
$\mu^2(n)$ intentionally kills all nonsquarefree targets; the resulting port
is therefore adapted to a rough $P_1\cup P_2$ carrier rather than to the
complete von Mangoldt sequence.

For $j\ge1$ define the generalized von Mangoldt function
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_j(n)
 :=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)
       \left(\log\frac nd\right)^j.
 \label{eq:v003-generalized-lambda}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact Mellin-degree coefficients]\label{thm:v003-mellin-degree}
For every $j\ge1$ and every $n\ge2$,
\[
 \boxed{
 B_{j,X}(n)
 =\frac{\mu^2(n)\Lambda_j(n)}{j!L_X^j}.}
\]
In particular,
\[
 B_{1,X}(n)=\frac{\mu^2(n)\Lambda(n)}{L_X},
 \qquad
 B_{2,X}(n)=\frac{\mu^2(n)\Lambda_2(n)}{2L_X^2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $n$ is squarefree, then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{j\ge0}\Lambda_j(n)\frac{z^j}{j!L_X^j}
 &=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)
   \exp\!\left(\frac z{L_X}\log\frac nd\right)\\
 &=n^{z/L_X}\prod_{\ell\mid n}(1-\ell^{-z/L_X})
 =\prod_{\ell\mid n}(\ell^{z/L_X}-1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Coefficient comparison proves the formula.  If $n$ is nonsquarefree, both
sides vanish after multiplication by $\mu^2(n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Exact degree-$\le2$ squarefree port]
Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak P_{\le2,X}(n):=B_{1,X}(n)+B_{2,X}(n).
 \label{eq:v003-Ple2}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Support and exact values]\label{prop:v003-Ple2-values}
For $2\le n\le2X$,
\[
 \mathfrak P_{\le2,X}(n)=
 \begin{cases}
  \alpha+\alpha^2/2,
    &n=p\text{ prime},\\[1mm]
  \alpha_1\alpha_2,
    &n=p_1p_2,\ p_1\ne p_2\text{ prime},\\[1mm]
  0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
\]
where $\alpha=\log p/L_X$ and
$\alpha_i=\log p_i/L_X$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For one prime,
$\e^{\alpha z}-1=\alpha z+\alpha^2z^2/2+O(z^3)$.
For two distinct primes the product begins with
$\alpha_1\alpha_2z^2$.  A squarefree integer with at least three prime
factors has no coefficient of degree one or two, and a nonsquarefree integer
is killed by definition.
\end{proof}

\section{Atom--bulk decomposition}

\begin{lemma}[Selberg degree-two identity]\label{lem:v003-selberg}
For every $n\ge1$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda_2(n)=\Lambda(n)\log n+(\Lambda*\Lambda)(n).}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\Re s>1$, the Dirichlet series of $\Lambda_2$ is
$\zeta''(s)/\zeta(s)$.  Put $A(s)=-\zeta'(s)/\zeta(s)$.  The Dirichlet
series of $\Lambda(n)\log n$ is $-A'(s)$ and that of
$\Lambda*\Lambda$ is $A(s)^2$.  Since
\[
 -A'(s)+A(s)^2=\frac{\zeta''(s)}{\zeta(s)},
\]
uniqueness of absolutely convergent Dirichlet series gives the identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact prime atom plus semiprime bulk]\label{cor:v003-atom-bulk}
For every $n\ge2$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak P_{\le2,X}(n)
 ={}&\mu^2(n)\frac{\Lambda(n)}{L_X}
      \left(1+\frac{\log n}{2L_X}\right)\\
 &+\frac{\mu^2(n)}{2L_X^2}(\Lambda*\Lambda)(n).
 \end{aligned}}
\]
The first term is supported exactly on primes.  The second is supported
exactly on squarefree semiprimes and equals
$(\log r\log q)/L_X^2$ at $n=rq$ with $r\ne q$ prime.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Insert \Cref{thm:v003-mellin-degree,lem:v003-selberg}.  At a prime the
convolution term vanishes.  At a squarefree semiprime $rq$ it equals
$2\log r\log q$.  At every other squarefree depth the degree-two coefficient
vanishes.
\end{proof}

\section{A signed pointwise prime minorant}

For $2\le n\le2X$ put
\[
 \alpha_X(n):=\frac{\log n}{L_X}
\]
and define
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak J_X(n)
 :=\frac67\left(
 B_{1,X}(n)+B_{2,X}(n)
 -\frac{2}{\alpha_X(n)}B_{3,X}(n)
 \right).
 \label{eq:v003-signed-separator}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact cancellation of the squarefree $P_2$ layer]
\label{thm:v003-signed-separator}
For $2\le n\le2X$,
\[
 \mathfrak J_X(n)=
 \begin{cases}
  \dfrac67\alpha+\dfrac17\alpha^2,
     &n=p\text{ prime},\\[2mm]
  0,&n\text{ a squarefree semiprime},\\[1mm]
  -\dfrac{12}{7\alpha_X(n)}B_{3,X}(n)<0,
     &n\text{ squarefree with three prime factors},\\[2mm]
  0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathfrak J_X(n)\le\1_{\mathbb P}(n)}
 \label{eq:v003-prime-minorant}
\end{equation}
for every $2\le n\le2X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a prime, $B_1=\alpha$, $B_2=\alpha^2/2$, and
$B_3=\alpha^3/6$, which gives the first value.  Since $0<\alpha\le1$, it
lies in $(0,1]$.  For $n=pq$, write
$\alpha=\alpha_p+\alpha_q$.  Then
\[
 B_2=\alpha_p\alpha_q,
 \qquad
 B_3=\frac12\alpha_p\alpha_q(\alpha_p+\alpha_q)
     =\frac\alpha2B_2,
\]
so the separator vanishes.  At squarefree factor depth three one has
$B_1=B_2=0$ and $B_3>0$.  At depth at least four the first three
coefficients vanish.  Nonsquarefree targets vanish by definition.  The
pointwise minorant follows from this case division.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Signed fixed-gap and fixed-sum closure]
\label{cor:v003-signed-additive-closure}
Define
\[
 \mathscr J_{\rm twin}(X)
 :=\sum_{\substack{p\le X\\p\ \mathrm{prime}}}
   (\log p)\mathfrak J_X(p+2)
\]
and, for even $N$,
\[
 \mathscr J_{\rm Gold}(N)
 :=\sum_{\substack{p<N-1\\p\ \mathrm{prime}}}
   (\log p)\mathfrak J_N(N-p).
\]
If $\mathscr J_{\rm twin}(X)>0$ for an unbounded sequence of $X$, then
there are infinitely many twin primes.  If
$\mathscr J_{\rm Gold}(N)>0$ for every sufficiently large even $N$, then
binary Goldbach holds for all such $N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If no target $p+2$ were prime, every summand in
$\mathscr J_{\rm twin}(X)$ would be nonpositive by
\eqref{eq:v003-prime-minorant}.  Thus positivity forces at least one twin
pair.  The same argument with target $N-p$ proves the Goldbach statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The separator solves the algebraic $P_1/P_2$ problem exactly, but the
resulting shifted or fixed-sum correlation is signed.  Proving its positivity
is a new Type--II/parity estimate, not a consequence of the interpolation
formula.
\end{remark}

\chapter{Stable finite Cauchy realization of the degree-two carrier}

\section{A bounded finite evaluation bank}

Fix an integer $m\ge3$, let $\zeta_m=\e^{2\pi i/m}$, and choose
$0<r<s<1$.  For $j=1,2$ define
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak D^{(j)}_{m,r,X}(n)
 :=\frac1{mr^j}\sum_{v=0}^{m-1}
   \zeta_m^{-jv}\mathfrak E_{r\zeta_m^v,X}(n).
 \label{eq:v003-cauchy-D}
\end{equation}
Root-of-unity orthogonality gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak D^{(j)}_{m,r,X}(n)
 =\sum_{\ell\ge0}B_{j+\ell m,X}(n)r^{\ell m}.
 \label{eq:v003-cauchy-alias}
\end{equation}
Put
\[
 \vartheta:=\left(\frac rs\right)^m,
 \qquad
 C_s:=\frac1s+\frac1{s^2},
\]
and define
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak c^{(\le2)}_{m;r,s,X}(n)
 :=\mathfrak D^{(1)}_{m,r,X}(n)
  +\mathfrak D^{(2)}_{m,r,X}(n)
  -\vartheta C_s\mathfrak E_{s,X}(n).
 \label{eq:v003-cauchy-minorant}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Stable Cauchy minorant]\label{thm:v003-cauchy-minorant}
For every $n\ge2$,
\[
 \boxed{
 0\le
 \mathfrak P_{\le2,X}(n)-\mathfrak c^{(\le2)}_{m;r,s,X}(n)
 \le
 \vartheta C_s\mathfrak E_{s,X}(n).}
\]
In particular,
$\mathfrak c^{(\le2)}_{m;r,s,X}\le\mathfrak P_{\le2,X}$ pointwise.  The
total absolute coefficient weight of the finite evaluation bank is at most
\[
 \boxed{r^{-1}+r^{-2}+\vartheta C_s,}
\]
independently of $m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient of $B_{d,X}(n)$ in
\eqref{eq:v003-cauchy-minorant} is
\[
 \kappa_d
 =r^{d-1}\1_{d\equiv1\pmod m}
  +r^{d-2}\1_{d\equiv2\pmod m}
  -\vartheta C_ss^d.
\]
For $d=1,2$ one has $\kappa_d\le1$.  If
$d=1+\ell m$ with $\ell\ge1$, then
\[
 r^{d-1}=\vartheta^\ell s^{d-1}
 \le\vartheta C_ss^d
\]
because $C_ss=1+s^{-1}>1$.  The same argument for
$d=2+\ell m$ uses $C_ss^2=1+s>1$.  Every other coefficient of degree at
least three is nonpositive.  Since all $B_{d,X}(n)$ are nonnegative, this
proves the lower inequality and the pointwise minorant.  Subtracting the
aliased positive tails shows that the difference is at most
$\vartheta C_s\mathfrak E_{s,X}(n)$.  Finally, the two Fourier banks have
coefficient variations $r^{-1}$ and $r^{-2}$, and the correction contributes
$\vartheta C_s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dyadic bank]
For $r=1/4$ and $s=1/2$,
\[
 0\le\mathfrak P_{\le2,X}(n)
 -\mathfrak c^{(\le2)}_{m;1/4,1/2,X}(n)
 \le6\,2^{-m}\mathfrak E_{1/2,X}(n),
\]
and the total coefficient variation is at most $26$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Positive-mass transfer through the finite bank]
\label{thm:v003-cauchy-transfer}
Let $b_n\ge0$ be finitely supported weights and set
\[
 Q_{\le2}:=\sum_nb_n\mathfrak P_{\le2,X}(n),
 \qquad
 U_s:=\sum_nb_n\mathfrak E_{s,X}(n),
\]
\[
 C_m:=\sum_nb_n\mathfrak c^{(\le2)}_{m;r,s,X}(n).
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{Q_{\le2}-\vartheta C_sU_s\le C_m\le Q_{\le2}.}
\]
Consequently, any lower bound $Q_{\le2}\ge M_X$ survives in a finite
phase-complete tensor whenever $\vartheta C_sU_s=o(M_X)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiply the pointwise inequalities of
\Cref{thm:v003-cauchy-minorant} by $b_n$ and sum.
\end{proof}

\chapter{Prime--semiprime seed theorems and rigorous conditional transfers}

\section{Li--Liu inputs and exponent conversion}

The following qualitative statements are external inputs.  Li and Liu prove
in their terminology Proposition $(1-1.75)$ for twin primes and Proposition
$(1+1.9)$ for Goldbach \cite{LiLiu2026}: the target is either prime or a product $rq$ of two
primes with the indicated factor-ratio restriction.  Quantitative constants
and support refinements are kept separate below.

\begin{proposition}[Factor-ratio conversion]\label{prop:v003-ratio-conversion}
If $n=rq$ with $1\le r\le q^a$, then
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\log r}{\log n}\le\frac{a}{1+a},
 \qquad
 \frac{\log q}{\log n}\ge\frac1{1+a}.}
\]
Thus $a=3/4$ gives the interval endpoint $3/7$, and $a=9/10$ gives
$9/19$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The inequality $\log r\le a\log q$ gives
$\log n=\log r+\log q\le(1+a)\log q$.  This proves the second inequality;
the first follows by subtraction from one.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Quantitative prime--semiprime seeds]
\label{ass:v003-quant-seeds}
There are constants $c_{\rm T},c_{\rm G},\varepsilon>0$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(Q\arabic*)}]
\item for all sufficiently large $x$, at least
$c_{\rm T}\Sing(2)x/\log^2x$ primes
$p\in(\varepsilon x,x)$ satisfy
$p+2=rq$, where $q$ is prime, $r=1$ or prime, and $r\le q^{3/4}$;
\item for every sufficiently large even $N$, at least
$c_{\rm G}\Sing(N)N/\log^2N$ representations
$N=p+rq$ have $p,q$ prime, $r=1$ or prime, and $r\le q^{9/10}$.
\end{enumerate}
These are external quantitative inputs; they are not proved in this
monograph.
\end{assumption}

Define
\[
 \mathscr Q_{\rm T}^{(\le2)}(x)
 :=\sum_{\substack{p\le x\\p\ \mathrm{prime}}}
   (\log p)\mathfrak P_{\le2,x}(p+2)
\]
and
\[
 \mathscr Q_{\rm G}^{(\le2)}(N)
 :=\sum_{\substack{p<N\\p\ \mathrm{prime}}}
   (\log p)\mathfrak P_{\le2,N}(N-p).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative seed transfer]\label{thm:v003-seed-transfer}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:v003-quant-seeds},
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr Q_{\rm T}^{(\le2)}(x)
 \gg\Sing(2)\frac{x}{\log^2x}}
\]
for all sufficiently large $x$, and
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr Q_{\rm G}^{(\le2)}(N)
 \gg\Sing(N)\frac{N}{\log^2N}}
\]
uniformly for sufficiently large even $N$.

If, in addition, every composite target in the relevant seed satisfies
$r\ge x^\alpha$ in the twin case, respectively $r\ge N^\alpha$ in the
Goldbach case, for some fixed $\alpha>0$, then the corresponding lower bound
improves by one logarithm to
\[
 \mathscr Q_{\rm T}^{(\le2)}(x)
 \gg\Sing(2)\frac{x}{\log x},
 \qquad
 \mathscr Q_{\rm G}^{(\le2)}(N)
 \gg\Sing(N)\frac{N}{\log N}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the twin seed, $\log p\asymp\log x$.  A prime target contributes
$\gg\log x$.  For a composite target $n=rq>\varepsilon x$, one has
$r\ge2$ and, by \Cref{prop:v003-ratio-conversion},
$\log q\gg\log x$.  Hence
\[
 (\log p)\mathfrak P_{\le2,x}(rq)
 =(\log p)\frac{\log r\log q}{L_x^2}
 \gg1.
\]
Multiplication by the number of candidates proves the first bound.

For Goldbach, discard representations with
$p\le N^{1/2}$ or $rq\le N^{1/2}$; there are $O(N^{1/2})$, negligible
against $N/\log^2N$.  On the remainder, $\log p\gg\log N$ and
\Cref{prop:v003-ratio-conversion} gives $\log q\gg\log N$.  The same
calculation gives a positive absolute contribution for each composite
representation, while a prime target contributes $\gg\log N$.

Under the additional rough lower bound, both $\log r/L$ and $\log q/L$ are
bounded below by positive constants.  Every candidate then contributes
$\gg\log x$ or $\gg\log N$, yielding the stronger scale.
\end{proof}

\begin{warning}
The theorem proves positive prime-or-semiprime mass, not prime mass.  The
semiprime layer may carry the entire lower bound.  The exact separator
\eqref{eq:v003-signed-separator} or the parity current of
\Cref{thm:parity-recovery} still requires a signed arithmetic estimate.
\end{warning}

\section{A transparent support-retaining hypothesis}

Fix $0<\kappa<\tau<1/2$ and define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 w_\kappa(n;x):={}&
 \1_{P^-(n)\ge x^\kappa}
 -\1_{\Omega(n)\ge2}\1_{P^-(n)\ge x^\tau}\\
 &+\1_{\Omega(n)\ge3}\1_{P^-(n)\ge x^\tau}
 -\1_{\Omega(n)\ge3}\1_{P^-(n)\ge x^\kappa}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:v003-rough-weight}
\end{equation}
Here $P^-(n)$ is the least prime divisor and $\Omega(n)$ counts prime
factors with multiplicity.

\begin{lemma}[Pointwise support of the rough weight]
\label{lem:v003-rough-weight-support}
Suppose $n\asymp x$ and $x$ is sufficiently large.  Then
\[
 w_\kappa(n;x)=1
\]
exactly when either $n$ is prime, or $n=rq$ with distinct primes
$r<q$ and $x^\kappa\le r<x^\tau$; otherwise the weight is zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $P^-(n)<x^\kappa$, the first term vanishes and the two depth-$\ge3$
terms cancel, while depth at most two contributes zero.  If
$x^\kappa\le P^-(n)<x^\tau$, the value is one at factor depth two and
zero at depth at least three.  A depth-one integer in this range would be a
prime $n=P^-(n)<x^\tau$, contradicting $n\asymp x$.  In the depth-two
case, writing $n=rq$ with $r=P^-(n)$, the inequalities $n\asymp x$ and
$r<x^\tau$ with $\tau<1/2$ give $q>r$ for large $x$.  If
$P^-(n)\ge x^\tau$, the second term cancels the first at every composite
depth, with the third term restoring and the fourth term removing the
depth-$\ge3$ part; only primes remain.
\end{proof}

Let
\[
 \mathcal K=\left\{\frac1{12},\frac17,\frac27,\frac13\right\},
 \qquad
 \tau=\frac37-\varepsilon_0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal W_x(n)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal K}w_\kappa(n;x).
\]
Then $0\le\mathcal W_x\le4$, and its positive support consists of primes
and semiprimes $rq$ satisfying
\[
 x^{1/12}\le r<x^{3/7-\varepsilon_0}.
\]

\begin{assumption}[Support-retaining Li--Liu audit hypothesis]
\label{ass:v003-support-retaining}
For some $c_0>0$ and all sufficiently large $x$,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\varepsilon_0x<p<x\\p\ \mathrm{prime}}}
 \mathcal W_x(p+2)
 \ge c_0\Sing(2)\frac{x}{\log^2x}.
\]
This is the support-retaining left-side deduction proposed in the V061
working notes \cite{GoldbachV061}.  It is recorded here as a hypothesis, not as an externally
verified theorem.
\end{assumption}

\begin{corollary}[Natural-scale rough carrier]
\label{cor:v003-natural-rough-carrier}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:v003-support-retaining},
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr Q_{\rm T}^{(\le2)}(x)
 \gg\Sing(2)\frac{x}{\log x}.}
\]
Moreover the positive mass comes entirely from prime targets and semiprime
targets $rq$ with
$x^{1/12}\le r<x^{3/7-\varepsilon_0}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every supported prime or semiprime has $\log p\asymp L_x$,
$\log r/L_x\ge1/13$ for sufficiently large $x$, and
$\log q/L_x\ge1/2$.  Hence every supported target contributes
$\gg L_x$ to the weighted degree-$\le2$ correlation.  The support count is
at least one quarter of the weighted lower bound because
$0\le\mathcal W_x\le4$.  Multiplication gives the result.
\end{proof}

\chapter{The semiprime profile and the remaining parity wall}

\section{Local factor-ratio regularity}

For a twin-type semiprime target $p+2=rq$ with $r<q$ prime, put
\[
 u_x(r):=\frac{\log r}{L_x}
\]
and define the positive measure
\begin{equation}
 \mu_x^{(2)}
 :=\frac{L_x}{x}
 \sum_{\substack{\varepsilon_0x<p<x\\p\ \mathrm{prime}\\
                   p+2=rq,\ r<q\ \mathrm{prime}}}
 (\log p)\frac{\log r}{L_x}\frac{\log q}{L_x}
 \delta_{u_x(r)}.
 \label{eq:v003-semiprime-profile}
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Standard two-linear-form upper-bound sieve]
\label{ass:v003-two-form-sieve}
Uniformly for odd primes $r\le(2x)^{1/2}$,
\[
 \#\left\{q:\ q\ \mathrm{prime},\ rq-2\ \mathrm{prime},\
             \varepsilon_0x<rq-2<x\right\}
 \ll_{\varepsilon_0}\frac{x}{rL_x^2}.
\]
This is the standard dimension-two upper-bound sieve for the primitive
linear forms $q$ and $rq-2$; see, for example, \cite{IwaniecKowalski}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Lipschitz semiprime profile]\label{thm:v003-profile-lipschitz}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:v003-two-form-sieve}, uniformly for
$0\le u<v\le1/2$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \mu_x^{(2)}([u,v])
 \ll_{\varepsilon_0}v-u+\frac1{L_x}.}
\]
Every weak-* subsequential limit of $\mu_x^{(2)}$ is therefore absolutely
continuous with an essentially bounded density.  In particular, the
normalized degree-two mass has no atom at a fixed factor ratio.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $r$, the sieve bound and
$(\log p)(\log q)/L_x^2\le1$ show that the unnormalized contribution is
\[
 \ll\frac{x\log r}{rL_x^2}.
\]
Sum over primes $r$ with
$\e^{uL_x}<r\le\e^{vL_x}$ and use
\[
 \sum_{y<r\le z\atop r\ \mathrm{prime}}\frac{\log r}{r}
 \ll\log(z/y)+1.
\]
After multiplying by $L_x/x$, the stated interval bound follows.  Uniform
interval domination implies absolute continuity and an $L^\infty$ density
for every weak-* limit.
\end{proof}

\section{Atomic prime fibre versus continuous semiprime fibre}

Define the normalized twin atom
\[
 \tau_x:=\frac{L_x}{x}
 \sum_{\substack{\varepsilon_0x<p<x\\p,p+2\ \mathrm{prime}}}
 (\log p)
 \left[
  \frac{\log(p+2)}{L_x}
  +\frac12\left(\frac{\log(p+2)}{L_x}\right)^2
 \right].
\]
Let $\mu_x^\sharp$ be the restriction of
\eqref{eq:v003-semiprime-profile} to
$[1/12,3/7-\varepsilon_0]$ and put
\[
 \nu_x^\sharp:=\tau_x\delta_0+\mu_x^\sharp.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Atomic--continuous alternative]
\label{thm:v003-atomic-continuous}
Assume Assumptions~\ref{ass:v003-support-retaining} and~\ref{ass:v003-two-form-sieve}.  Then
\[
 \nu_x^\sharp([0,1/2])\gg\Sing(2)
\]
for all sufficiently large $x$.  Every weak-* subsequential limit has the
form
\[
 \boxed{\nu=\tau\delta_0+f(u)\dd u,}
\]
where $f\in L^\infty$, $\supp f\subset[1/12,3/7]$, and
$\tau+\int f\gg\Sing(2)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The total mass is exactly the normalization by $L_x/x$ of the rough
prime-plus-semiprime contribution in
\Cref{cor:v003-natural-rough-carrier}.  The semiprime part has uniformly
bounded density by \Cref{thm:v003-profile-lipschitz}; its support converges
into $[1/12,3/7]$.  The prime contribution is concentrated at the isolated
endpoint $u=0$.  Weak-* compactness of bounded positive measures gives the
claimed decomposition and mass lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{warning}
The theorem does not force $\tau>0$.  All the positive mass may converge to
a bounded continuous semiprime profile.  Twin primes require a contraction
of that profile, a positive signed separator correlation, or an equivalent
M\"obius-current estimate.
\end{warning}

\chapter{Selector firewalls and the first genuinely quadratic targets}

\section{First moments cannot select a twin edge}

Let $\mathcal H=\{h_1,\ldots,h_k\}$ and let $G_2(\mathcal H)$ join $i$ and
$j$ when $|h_i-h_j|=2$.  This graph is bipartite: color $h$ according to
whether $h\bmod4$ belongs to $\{0,1\}$ or to $\{2,3\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Twin-free realization of all sub-half marginals]
\label{thm:v003-twinfree-marginals}
If $0\le\pi_i\le1/2$, there is a probability distribution supported on
independent sets of $G_2(\mathcal H)$ whose vertex marginals are exactly
$\pi_i$.  Hence no statistic depending only linearly on the one-coordinate
marginals can certify a twin edge.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $A\sqcup B$ be the bipartition.  Choose $A$ or $B$ with probability
$1/2$.  Conditional on the chosen side, include vertex $i$ independently
with probability $2\pi_i$ and include no vertex on the other side.  The
result is an independent set and vertex $i$ is included with probability
$\pi_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Maynard coordinate ceiling]
\label{prop:v003-maynard-ceiling}
For a standard Maynard test function $F$ \cite{Maynard2015}, the coordinate integrals satisfy
$J_k^{(i)}(F)\le I_k(F)$.  Thus the standard first-moment asymptotic
\[
 \pi_i=\frac{\log R}{\log N}\frac{J_k^{(i)}(F)}{I_k(F)}+o(1),
 \qquad R=N^{\theta/2-\delta},
\]
gives $\limsup\pi_i\le\theta/2-\delta<1/2$.  Consequently the complete
first-moment data are compatible with the twin-free model of
\Cref{thm:v003-twinfree-marginals}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed values of the other variables, Cauchy--Schwarz in the $i$th
coordinate gives
\[
 \left(\int F\,\dd t_i\right)^2\le\int F^2\,\dd t_i.
\]
Integrating over the remaining variables yields $J_k^{(i)}(F)\le I_k(F)$.
Substitution proves the ceiling, and
\Cref{thm:v003-twinfree-marginals} gives the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\section{The singular series is an anti-selector}

\begin{proposition}[Gap $2$ has minimal even-gap singular series]
\label{prop:v003-minimal-singular-series}
For every nonzero even integer $d$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \Sing(d)=\Sing(2)
 \prod_{\ell\mid d,\ \ell>2}\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}
 \ge\Sing(2).}
\]
Equality holds exactly when $d$ has no odd prime divisor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each factor $(\ell-1)/(\ell-2)$ is greater than one.  The product is empty
exactly when no odd prime divides $d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Local Euler densities therefore do not privilege the twin edge; among even
gaps the twin singular series is minimal, with equality shared by powers of
two.  A successful selector must use global two-coordinate information.
\end{remark}

\section{Matching energy}

For binary variables $X_j,Y_j\in\{0,1\}$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{2X_jY_j=X_j+Y_j-(X_j-Y_j)^2.}
 \label{eq:v003-matching-identity}
\end{equation}
For nonnegative weights $w_n$ put
\[
 \mathcal V_w=\sum_nw_n\sum_j(X_j(n)+Y_j(n)),
\]
\[
 \mathcal D_w=\sum_nw_n\sum_j(X_j(n)-Y_j(n))^2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_w=\sum_nw_n\sum_jX_j(n)Y_j(n).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact matching-energy decomposition]
\label{prop:v003-matching-energy}
One has
\[
 \boxed{2\mathcal E_w=\mathcal V_w-\mathcal D_w.}
\]
Thus first moments estimate $\mathcal V_w$, while a genuine twin selector
requires a strict contraction of the imbalance energy $\mathcal D_w$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Multiply \eqref{eq:v003-matching-identity} by $w_n$, sum over $n$ and $j$,
and collect terms.
\end{proof}

\section{Negative-temperature parity is not a new route}

\begin{proposition}[Exact positive/negative temperature identity]
\label{prop:v003-negative-temperature}
For every real $a$, every $X\ge2$, and every $2\le n\le2X$,
\[
 \boxed{
 n^{a/L_X}\mathfrak E_{-a,X}(n)
 =\mu(n)\mathfrak E_{a,X}(n).}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \frac{\mathfrak E_{a,X}(n)
       -n^{a/L_X}\mathfrak E_{-a,X}(n)}{2a}
 =\frac{1-\mu(n)}{2a}\mathfrak E_{a,X}(n).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For nonsquarefree $n$, both sides vanish.  If
$n=p_1\cdots p_k$ is squarefree and
$\alpha_j=\log p_j/L_X$, then
\[
 \prod_j(\e^{-a\alpha_j}-1)
 =(-1)^k\e^{-a\sum_j\alpha_j}
   \prod_j(\e^{a\alpha_j}-1)
 =\mu(n)n^{-a/L_X}\mathfrak E_{a,X}(n).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Parity firewall]
On a squarefree $P_1\cup P_2$ carrier the temperature difference separates
primes and semiprimes exactly, but every shifted or fixed-sum estimate of it
contains the weighted M\"obius current linearly.  It is therefore a rewriting
of the parity problem, not an independent positive argument.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Insert the second identity into the relevant source-prime sum.  The term
with $1$ is a positive-temperature moment; the second is precisely the
weighted correlation with $\mu$ of the shifted or complementary target.
\end{proof}

\part{The common mass--prefix coercivity engine}

\chapter{Gaussian kernel descent}

\section{Discrete mass and prefix current}

Let
\[
 s_0<s_1<\cdots<s_N
\]
be predetermined logarithmic cell centres and let $d_0,\ldots,d_N$ be Hilbert-valued discrepancy coefficients.  Define
\[
 M(d)=\sum_{c=0}^Nd_c,
 \qquad
 q_c(d)=\sum_{r=0}^cd_r\quad(0\le c<N).
\]
For a port $F\in L^2(\R)$ and translations $T_sF(u)=F(u+s)$, put
\[
 \mathcal C_F(d)=\sum_{c=0}^Nd_c\otimes T_{s_c}F.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact mass--prefix identity]\label{thm:mass-prefix}
One has
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_F(d)
 =M(d)\otimes T_{s_N}F
 +\sum_{c=0}^{N-1}q_c(d)\otimes
 \bigl(T_{s_c}F-T_{s_{c+1}}F\bigr).}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient of $T_{s_0}F$ is $q_0=d_0$.  For $1\le c<N$ it is $q_c-q_{c-1}=d_c$, and the coefficient of $T_{s_N}F$ is $M-q_{N-1}=d_N$.
\end{proof}

Define the step prefix field
\[
 J_d(s)=q_c(d)\quad(s_c\le s<s_{c+1}),
\]
with $J_d=0$ outside $[s_0,s_N)$.

\begin{lemma}[Fourier boundary identity]\label{lem:fourier-boundary}
With the unitary Fourier transform,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{c=0}^N\e^{its_c}d_c
 =\e^{its_N}M(d)-it\sqrt{2\pi}\,\widehat J_d(t).}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Compute
\[
 -it\int_{s_0}^{s_N}\e^{its}J_d(s)\dd s
 =\sum_{c=0}^{N-1}q_c
 \bigl(\e^{its_c}-\e^{its_{c+1}}\bigr)
 =\sum_{c=0}^N\e^{its_c}d_c-\e^{its_N}M(d).
\]
Insert the unitary normalization.
\end{proof}

\section{Port-adapted \texorpdfstring{$H^{-1}$}{H-minus-one} control}

Set
\[
 \Lambda_F=\sup_{t\in\R}t^2|\widehat F(t)|^2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Mass--flux inequality]\label{thm:mass-flux}
If $\Lambda_F<\infty$, then
\[
 \boxed{
 \norm{\mathcal C_F(d)}
 \le\norm F\,\norm{M(d)}
 +\sqrt{2\pi\Lambda_F}\,
 \norm{J_d}_{L^2}.}
\]
If $M(d)=0$, then
\[
 \boxed{
 \norm{\mathcal C_F(d)}^2
 =2\pi\int_{\R}t^2|\widehat F(t)|^2
 \norm{\widehat J_d(t)}^2\dd t.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Plancherel,
\[
 \norm{\mathcal C_F(d)}^2
 =\int_{\R}|\widehat F(t)|^2
 \left\|\sum_c\e^{its_c}d_c\right\|^2\dd t.
\]
Use \Cref{lem:fourier-boundary}, the triangle inequality, and Plancherel for $J_d$.  When $M=0$, the boundary term vanishes and the identity is exact.
\end{proof}

For the normalized Gaussian satisfying
\[
 |\widehat F_{\mathfrak a}(t)|^2=\e^{-2\mathfrak a t^2},
\]
one has
\[
 \Lambda_{F_{\mathfrak a}}=\frac{1}{2\mathfrak a e}.
\]
Hence
\[
 \boxed{
 \norm{\mathcal C_{F_{\mathfrak a}}(d)}
 \le\norm{F_{\mathfrak a}}\norm{M(d)}
 +\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\mathfrak a e}}\norm{J_d}_{L^2}.}
\]

\section{A common source for many ports}

Let $F_1,\ldots,F_R$ be a preassigned finite collection of spectral and additive ports and let $\lambda_r>0$.  The same discrepancy packet $d$ defines every synthesis $\mathcal C_{F_r}(d)$.

\begin{theorem}[Multiport mass--prefix theorem]\label{thm:multiport-prefix}
One has
\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \left[\sum_r\lambda_r\|\mathcal C_{F_r}(d)\|^2\right]^{1/2}
 \le{}&
 \left[\sum_r\lambda_r\|F_r\|^2\right]^{1/2}\|M(d)\|\\
 &+\left[2\pi\sum_r\lambda_r\Lambda_{F_r}\right]^{1/2}
 \|J_d\|_{L^2}.
 \end{aligned}}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{thm:mass-flux} in the Hilbert direct sum $\bigoplus_r\mathcal H_r$ after replacing $F_r$ by $\sqrt{\lambda_r}F_r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Joint flagship source quotient]
For preassigned source-to-port maps $C_r$, define
\[
 K_{\rm joint}=\sum_r\lambda_rC_r^*C_r.
\]
Then
\[
 \ker K_{\rm joint}=\bigcap_r\ker C_r.
\]
The quotient by this common nullspace is the smallest phase-complete history source visible to every selected port.
\end{definition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the nullspace identity]
If $x\in\ker K_{\rm joint}$, positivity gives
\[
 0=\ip{x}{K_{\rm joint}x}=\sum_r\lambda_r\|C_rx\|^2,
\]
so every $C_rx=0$.  The converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\chapter{Unified conditional arithmetic closure}

\begin{theorem}[Grand arithmetic closure theorem]\label{thm:grand-arithmetic}
Fix a finite collection of the following preassigned ports:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item cutoff-matched Gaussian character ports at lines $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_m$;
\item the normal difference port at $h=2$;
\item anomalous fixed-sum ports for selected even integers $N$;
\item factor-depth or M\"obius marked ports on independently verified $P_1\cup P_2$ carriers;
\item the signed degree-separator ports $\mathfrak J_X(p+2)$ and $\mathfrak J_N(N-p)$ of \Cref{thm:v003-signed-separator}.
\end{enumerate}
Suppose the direct--actual and actual--canonical branch discrepancies of these ports factor through one predetermined logarithmic cell packet.  If, in the normalization required by each port,
\[
 \|M(d)\|=o(A_X),
 \qquad
 \|J_d\|_{L^2}=o(A_X),
\]
then every selected endpoint port is $o(A_X)$.

The following consequences hold after inserting the sector-specific hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item on a fixed subcritical line, the spectral conclusion is the corresponding zero strip;

\section{Finite Lorentzian Clifford core}

Let $J,S$ be self-adjoint involutions on a binary order plane with
\[
 J^2=S^2=I,
 \qquad
 JS=-SJ.
\]
Let $\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3$ be the Pauli matrices.  On the tensor product define
\[
 \gamma^0=J\otimes I_2,
 \qquad
 \gamma^i=JS\otimes\sigma_i.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite Lorentzian Clifford theorem]\label{thm:clifford}
The matrices satisfy
\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu+\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu
 =2\eta^{\mu\nu}I_4,}
\]
with $\eta=\diag(1,-1,-1,-1)$.  Consequently they generate
\[
 \Cl_{1,3}(\C)\cong M_4(\C).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Clearly $(\gamma^0)^2=I$.  Since $JSJS=-I$, $(\gamma^i)^2=-I$.  The anticommutation of $J$ with $JS$ gives $\{\gamma^0,\gamma^i\}=0$, while the Pauli relations give $\{\gamma^i,\gamma^j\}=-2\delta_{ij}I$.  The complex Clifford algebra in four generators is the full matrix algebra $M_4(\C)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Dirac scaling from accumulated histories}

On the lattice $h\Z^3$, define centered differences
\[
 (\nabla_{h,j}\psi)(x)=\frac{\psi(x+he_j)-\psi(x-he_j)}{2h}
\]
and the lattice Dirac Hamiltonian
\[
 D_h=-i\sum_{j=1}^3\alpha^j\nabla_{h,j},
 \qquad
 \alpha^j=\gamma^0\gamma^j.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Flat Dirac scaling]\label{thm:dirac-scaling}
For every Schwartz spinor $\psi$ on $\R^3$, after sampling on $h\Z^3$ and identifying the lattice with piecewise-constant functions,
\[
 D_h\psi\longrightarrow -i\sum_j\alpha^j\partial_j\psi
\]
in $L^2$ as $h\downarrow0$.  The corresponding self-adjoint operators converge in strong resolvent sense on the standard core.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Taylor expansion gives
\[
 \nabla_{h,j}\psi=\partial_j\psi+O(h^2)
\]
uniformly with all Schwartz weights.  Hence $D_h\psi\to D\psi$ in $L^2$ on a core.  The operators are self-adjoint Fourier multipliers with symbols
\[
 \sum_j\alpha^j\frac{\sin(hp_j)}h
\]
converging pointwise to $\sum_j\alpha^jp_j$ and uniformly on compact momentum sets.  Standard core convergence for self-adjoint operators yields strong resolvent convergence.
\end{proof}

\section{Screen entropy}

Let a stationary finite-state screen chain have stationary law $\pi$ and transition matrix $P$.  Its entropy rate is
\[
 h(P)=-\sum_{i,j}\pi_iP_{ij}\log P_{ij}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Markov screen area law]\label{prop:screen-area}
For a chain of $N$ cells,
\[
 H(X_1,\ldots,X_N)=H(\pi)+(N-1)h(P).
\]
If the screen has one cell per area $a_0$, then
\[
 S=\frac{h(P)}{a_0}\operatorname{Area}+O(1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The chain rule and the Markov property give
\[
 H(X_1^N)=H(X_1)+\sum_{k=2}^NH(X_k\mid X_{k-1})
 =H(\pi)+(N-1)h(P).
\]
Substitute $N=\operatorname{Area}/a_0+O(1)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Einstein closure from local horizon thermodynamics}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Einstein closure]\label{thm:einstein}
Assume a Lorentzian scaling limit with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(E\arabic*)}]
\item every spacetime point and null direction admits a local Rindler horizon with approximate boost generator and Unruh temperature $T=\kappa/(2\pi)$;
\item the horizon entropy variation is $\delta S=\eta\,\delta A$ with constant finite density $\eta>0$;
\item the heat flux is $\delta Q=\int T_{ab}\chi^a\dd\Sigma^b$ and satisfies $\delta Q=T\delta S$ for every local horizon;
\item the null congruence obeys Raychaudhuri's equation and the state has vanishing expansion and shear at the base point;
\item $\nabla^aT_{ab}=0$ and the contracted Bianchi identity holds.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=8\pi G_{\rm N}T_{ab},
 \qquad
 G_{\rm N}=\frac{1}{4\eta},}
\]
for a constant $\Lambda$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $k^a$ be the horizon generator and $\lambda$ an affine parameter vanishing at the base point.  Raychaudhuri's equation gives, to leading order,
\[
 \delta A=-\int\lambda R_{ab}k^ak^b\dd\lambda\dd A.
\]
The approximate boost field is $\chi^a=-\kappa\lambda k^a$, so
\[
 \delta Q=-\kappa\int\lambda T_{ab}k^ak^b\dd\lambda\dd A.
\]
Using $T=\kappa/(2\pi)$ and $\delta Q=T\eta\delta A$ yields
\[
 R_{ab}k^ak^b=\frac{2\pi}{\eta}T_{ab}k^ak^b
\]
for every null $k$.  Hence
\[
 R_{ab}+\Phi g_{ab}=\frac{2\pi}{\eta}T_{ab}
\]
for a scalar field $\Phi$.  Taking the divergence, using stress conservation and the contracted Bianchi identity, gives
\[
 \Phi=-\frac12R+\Lambda.
\]
Thus
\[
 G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=\frac{2\pi}{\eta}T_{ab}.
\]
Identifying $8\pi G_{\rm N}=2\pi/\eta$ gives the stated value.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite qutrit revision algebra}

Let $\mathbb F_3^2$ carry the symplectic form
\[
 [(p,q),(p',q')]=qp'-pq'.
\]
Let $\omega=\e^{2\pi i/3}$ and suppose a projective revision section satisfies
\[
 R_aR_b=\tau(a,b)R_{a+b},
 \qquad
 R_aR_bR_a^*R_b^*=\omega^{[a,b]}I.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Nondegenerate qutrit completion]\label{thm:qutrit}
If the commutator bicharacter is nondegenerate, then the $C^*$-algebra generated by the nine revision operators is
\[
 \boxed{C^*(R_a:a\in\mathbb F_3^2)\cong M_3(\C).}
\]
Its minimal faithful irreducible carrier has dimension three.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $a\ne0$, choose $c$ with $[c,a]\ne0$.  Conjugation gives
\[
 R_cR_aR_c^*=\omega^{[c,a]}R_a,
\]
so $\Tr R_a=0$.  Similarly,
\[
 \Tr(R_a^*R_b)=d\,\delta_{ab},
\]
where $d$ is the carrier dimension.  Hence the nine operators are linearly independent.  Their span is already a $*$-algebra because the projective law closes products and adjoints, so the generated algebra has dimension exactly nine.  A central element $\sum_ac_aR_a$ must satisfy $c_a\omega^{[b,a]}=c_a$ for every $b$, forcing $a=0$ whenever $c_a\ne0$.  Thus the center is scalar.  A finite-dimensional complex $C^*$-algebra with scalar center is one full matrix algebra $M_n(\C)$; its dimension is nine, so $n=3$.
\end{proof}

\end{enumerate}

\chapter{A common-source coupling lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Physical coupling controls a Gaussian port]
Let $(\Omega,\lambda)$ be a finite measure space, let $x:\Omega\to\mathcal K$ be Bochner integrable, and let $u,v:\Omega\to\R$.  Define
\[
 \mu_u=\int x(\zeta)\delta_{u(\zeta)}\dd\lambda(\zeta),
 \qquad
 \mu_v=\int x(\zeta)\delta_{v(\zeta)}\dd\lambda(\zeta).
\]
For the Gaussian port $F_{\mathfrak a}$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathcal C_{F_{\mathfrak a}}(\mu_u-\mu_v)\|^2
 \le
 \frac{\pi}{\mathfrak a e}
 \left(\int\|x\|^2\dd\lambda\right)
 \left(\int|u-v|\dd\lambda\right).}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each $\zeta$, the difference of two point masses generates a prefix field equal to $x(\zeta)$ on the interval between $u(\zeta)$ and $v(\zeta)$, with sign determined by orientation.  Minkowski and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \|J\|_{L^2}
 \le\int\|x(\zeta)\||u(\zeta)-v(\zeta)|^{1/2}\dd\lambda
 \le
 \left(\int\|x\|^2\dd\lambda\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\int|u-v|\dd\lambda\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The total mass vanishes, so apply the Gaussian specialization of \Cref{thm:mass-flux} and square.
\end{proof}

\begin{warning}
The coupling in this lemma must be supplied by the physical common source before the endpoint is evaluated.  An optimal transport coupling selected after observing the two endpoint measures is a post hoc comparison and does not establish source-level naturality.
\end{warning}
