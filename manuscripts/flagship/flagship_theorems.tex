\begin{abstract}
This note states the current renewal-spacetime construction as one self-contained
conditional theorem architecture.  The most primitive formulation begins with
composable operational processes and identifies histories that no admissible
future experiment can distinguish.  A shorter entry begins directly with a
finite complete resolved operator instrument.  Both routes lead to a finite
complex predictive algebra acted on by completely positive renewal maps.

A distinguished recurrent Store--Read phase then supplies the finite structures
needed for Lorentzian geometry.  A coherent binary Store interaction and its
retained completed records generate a nontrivial determinant orientation line;
reversible predictive units generate a projective commutator form; pressure on
a branching positive-depth recurrent graph selects an affine renewal clock; and
the marked primitive revision sector contains a minimal external
$M_4(\C)\cong\Cl_4(\C)$ factor.  Its canonical resolved direction instrument
has three external spatial directions and a positive second moment.  Under
controlled scaling, the resulting signed Hamiltonians converge to flat or
slowly varying Lorentzian Dirac dynamics.  In a primitive common-source
exchange phase, translational plaquette defects cancel while rotational
holonomy remains free, selecting Levi--Civita spin transport without imposing
flatness.

The theorem is a universality-class statement rather than a parameter-free
prediction of one universe.  The note lists every foundational, microscopic,
phase, analytic, selection, and metrological premise used by the current
formulation and gives a direct assessment of which premises remain physically
selective or potentially fit-like.  The central conclusion is a minimal
external $(3+1)$ Lorentzian core and controlled Dirac kinematics; uniqueness of
the total primitive dimension, a unique macroscopic metric, global topology,
an intrinsic choice of temporal orientation, Einstein dynamics, and matter
content are not asserted.
\end{abstract}

The theorem uses no prior manifold, metric tensor, causal cone, time coordinate,
spin structure, or spacetime dimension in the construction of the predictive
quotient, the signed cover, the projective revision algebra, or the renewal
pressure root.  The Lorentzian principal symbol and the limiting Dirac
Hamiltonian are reconstructed from finite predictive data.

The theorem nevertheless depends on a model being in a particular recurrent

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{finite predictive Store--Read data}\
\Longrightarrow\
\text{Lorentzian Dirac geometry in a selected scaling phase}
\end{gathered}}
\]
It does not mean that the phase itself is forced by bare composition or that
all microscopic data are uniquely determined.

\begin{definition}[Six logical roles]
Every premise in the theorem chain belongs to one of the following classes.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=2.8em]
\item A \emph{foundational reconstruction condition} is used only when the
      finite complex operator description is itself reconstructed from a
      prelinear operational theory.
\item A \emph{microscopic model datum} specifies the concrete finite process:
      local Store coefficients, resolved branches, depths, capacities, record
      transports, and control operations.
\item A \emph{phase criterion} is a checkable property of the specified model,
      such as recurrent branching, coherent binary coupling, determinant-odd
      record transport, reversible accessibility, or exchange covariance.
\item An \emph{analytic scaling condition} controls a continuum limit, including
      finite range, uniform ellipticity, coefficient regularity, common cores,
      form convergence, and bounded subprincipal terms.
\item A \emph{physical selection} chooses one recurrent component, boundary
      condition, zero-source state, orientation sector, or scaling net when
      several admissible choices coexist.
\item A \emph{metrological calibration} converts dimensionless renewal depth
      into conventional time, length, or energy units.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Fit-like premise]
A premise is called \emph{fit-like} when its content is tailored mainly to force
an intended target---for example exactly one negative direction, exactly three
spatial directions, a round spatial metric, a positive clock exponent, or
vanishing torsion---rather than describing an independently motivated
microscopic phase or an ordinary analytic domain.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Restrictive is not synonymous with fit-like]
A phase criterion may be restrictive without being a numerical or structural
fit.  A coherent Store interaction, a noncycle recurrent graph, or a
common-source exchange law excludes consistent counterphases, but none of these
conditions explicitly inserts a Lorentzian metric or the number three.  The
fit-risk assessment in \Cref{sec:fit-risk} therefore records both the degree of
physical selectivity and how directly a premise resembles the desired
conclusion.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Common-core strong-resolvent criterion]
\label{lem:app-core-resolvent}
Let $A_n$ and $A$ be self-adjoint operators on one Hilbert space $\Hh$.  Let
$\mathcal C\subset\Dom A$ be a core for $A$, assume
$\mathcal C\subset\Dom A_n$ for every $n$, and suppose
\[
  A_nu\longrightarrow Au
  \qquad (u\in\mathcal C).
\]
Then $A_n\to A$ in strong-resolvent sense.  Consequently
$f(A_n)\to f(A)$ strongly for every $f\in C_0(\R)$, and
$e^{-itA_n}\to e^{-itA}$ strongly, uniformly for $t$ in compact intervals.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $z\in\C\setminus\R$.  The map
$A-z:\Dom A\to\Hh$ is continuous for the graph norm and is onto because $A$
is self-adjoint.  Since $\mathcal C$ is graph-norm dense in $\Dom A$, its image
$(A-z)\mathcal C$ is therefore dense in $(A-z)\Dom A=\Hh$.

For $y=(A-z)u$ with $u\in\mathcal C$, the resolvent identity on the vector $u$
gives
\[
 (A_n-z)^{-1}y-u
 =(A_n-z)^{-1}(A-A_n)u.
\]
Since $\|(A_n-z)^{-1}\|\le |\im z|^{-1}$ and $(A_n-A)u\to0$, the right-hand
side tends to zero.  But $u=(A-z)^{-1}y$, so the resolvents converge on the
dense set $(A-z)\mathcal C$.  The same uniform resolvent bound extends the
convergence to every $y\in\Hh$.  This is strong-resolvent convergence.  The
$C_0$ functional calculus and compact-time unitary-group convergence are the
standard spectral-theorem consequences of strong-resolvent convergence.
\end{proof}

most one, so $d-1\le1$.  The case $d=1$ gives $S^0$, on which a connected group
cannot act transitively, and the classical case $d=0$ is excluded.  Therefore
$d=2$.  The finite Jordan classification then leaves only
$M_n(\C)_{\rm sa}$ on every nonclassical simple block; one-dimensional blocks
are classical.  The consistent orientation in \textup{(O4)} chooses one of the
two opposite associative products on each connected complex block.  Thus
\[
  \A_{\pred}\cong\bigoplus_a M_{n_a}(\C).
\]

\emph{Step 4: complete positivity.}
A deterministic operational transformation acts dually by a positive unital
map $\Phi$ between the reconstructed operator algebras.  Choose a qubit power
$2^m$ not smaller than any matrix-block size occurring in the source algebra.
By \textup{(O5)}, $\id_{M_{2^m}}\otimes\Phi$ is positive.  Compression to an
$n\times n$ matrix corner shows that $\id_{M_n}\otimes\Phi$ is positive for
each source block $M_n(\C)$.  Choi's finite-dimensional criterion therefore
makes $\Phi$ completely positive on every block and hence on the direct sum.
It is unital because deterministic discard is preserved.  Resolved outcomes
are represented by CP trace-nonincreasing branches whose outgoing sum is the
corresponding trace-preserving update.  Clause \textup{(O6)} either retains any
holistic fibre or proves that every external Store--Read observable factors
through the locally observable quotient, so no hidden composite degree of
freedom enters the external theorem chain.

The result is exactly a finite complex predictive algebra with a finite UCP
renewal alphabet, faithful reference states on the selected supports, and a
finite resolved CP instrument.  Under the direct operator entry, all of these
objects occur in the defining tuple $\mathfrak R$, so no reconstruction step is
needed.  The downstream theorems use only this common object, proving the final
claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Universal future quotient}

For a rooted history $h:I\to B$, define its future profile by
\[
  \Pi_B(h)(r,e):=\Pr(e\circ r\circ h).
\]
Future equivalence is equality of these functions.

\begin{lemma}[Future congruence and universal minimality]
\label{lem:app-future-minimality}
Future equivalence is an equivalence relation stable under admissible
postcomposition.  Its quotient $\Qq_+(B)$ is the unique reduced
future-sufficient memory, up to unique isomorphism fixing the encoded histories.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equality of functions is an equivalence relation.  If $h\sim_+h'$ and
$k:B\to C$ is admissible, then for every later continuation $d$ and terminal
test $e$,
\[
 \Pr(e\circ d\circ k\circ h)
 =\Pr(e\circ(d\circ k)\circ h)
 =\Pr(e\circ(d\circ k)\circ h')
 =\Pr(e\circ d\circ k\circ h'),
\]
so $k\circ h\sim_+k\circ h'$.

Let $X(B)$ be any future-sufficient realization with surjective encoding
$\eta_B$.  Define
\[
  \pi_B^X(\eta_B(h)):=[h].
\]
If $\eta_B(h)=\eta_B(h')$, transition compatibility and equality of all output
functions imply $\Pi_B(h)=\Pi_B(h')$, so the formula is well defined.  It is
surjective and intertwines all continuations and outputs.  If $X$ is reduced,
equal quotient classes have equal future outputs and hence represent the same
point of $X$, so $\pi_B^X$ is injective.  Conversely injectivity of $\pi_B^X$
implies reducedness.  Uniqueness follows from surjectivity of the encoding.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conditional successors and scalar renewal}

For a successful resolved history $h$, put
$p(h)=\Pr(u\circ h)>0$ and
\[
  \Pi^{\rm cond}(h)(F):=\frac{\Pr(F\circ h)}{p(h)}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Resolved successor congruence]
\label{lem:app-successor}
If $h$ and $h'$ have the same conditional future profile and a resolved branch
$e$ succeeds after both, then $e\circ h$ and $e\circ h'$ have the same
conditional future profile.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every future test $F$ after $e$, conditional equivalence applied to
$F\circ e$ and to the discard after $e$ gives
\[
 \frac{\Pr(F\circ e\circ h)}{p(h)}
 =\frac{\Pr(F\circ e\circ h')}{p(h')},
 \qquad
 \frac{p(e\circ h)}{p(h)}
 =\frac{p(e\circ h')}{p(h')}.
\]
The second common value is positive.  Dividing the first equality by the second
is exactly conditional equivalence of the extended histories.
\end{proof}

It follows that the observed branch $e$ defines a partial successor map
$\delta_e([h])=[e\circ h]$ on the conditional quotient.

\begin{lemma}[Reference-renewal identity]
\label{lem:app-reference-renewal}
In a reduced future-tomographic operator realization, every positive-weight
resolved edge $e:x\to y=\delta_e(x)$ satisfies
\[
  \mathcal E_e(\rho_x)=w_e\rho_y,
  \qquad w_e=\Tr\mathcal E_e(\rho_x).
\]
Consequently the weight of a path is the product of its edge weights.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The normalized branch output
$\widehat\rho_e=\mathcal E_e(\rho_x)/w_e$ represents the extended resolved
history.  By \Cref{lem:app-successor}, that history lies in the unique successor
class $y$.  Thus $\widehat\rho_e$ and $\rho_y$ agree on every admitted future
effect.  Future tomography separates reachable normalized states, so they are
equal.  Multiplying by $w_e$ proves the one-edge identity.  Induction along
$e_n\cdots e_1$ gives
\[
 \mathcal E_{e_n}\cdots\mathcal E_{e_1}(\rho_{x_0})
 =\left(\prod_{j=1}^n w_{e_j}\right)\rho_{x_n}.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Predictive units}

\begin{lemma}[A UCP unit is an automorphism]
\label{lem:app-ucp-unit}
If a UCP map $\Phi$ on a finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra has a UCP inverse
$\Psi$, then $\Phi$ is a $*$-automorphism.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Kadison--Schwarz gives
$\Phi(a)^*\Phi(a)\le\Phi(a^*a)$.  Applying $\Psi$ gives
\[
 \Psi(\Phi(a)^*\Phi(a))\le a^*a.
\]
Kadison--Schwarz for $\Psi$ at $\Phi(a)$ gives the reverse inequality,
\[
 a^*a=\Psi(\Phi(a))^*\Psi(\Phi(a))
 \le\Psi(\Phi(a)^*\Phi(a)).
\]
Hence equality holds.  Applying the same argument to $aa^*$ places every $a$ in
the multiplicative domain of $\Phi$, so $\Phi$ is a unital $*$-homomorphism.
Bijectivity makes it a $*$-automorphism.
\end{proof}

Accumulated UCP maps are closed under composition and contain the identity, so
they form a monoid; \Cref{lem:app-ucp-unit} proves the unit claim.

\subsection{Predictive-length spectral triple}

Let $E$ be the finite reset alphabet and let $\W=E^*/\!\sim$ be the quotient by
equality of accumulated predictive channels.  Put
\[
 \Lambda([w])=1+\min\{|v|:v\sim w\},
 \qquad
 D_+e_{[w]}=\Lambda([w])e_{[w]}.
\]
For a letter $\sigma$, let
$S_\sigma e_{[w]}=e_{[\sigma w]}$.

\begin{lemma}[Bounded shifts, commutators, and compact resolvent]
\label{lem:app-positive-triple}
If every $S_\sigma$ has uniformly finite fibres and
$|\Lambda([\sigma w])-\Lambda([w])|$ is uniformly bounded, then the algebra
generated by the shifts, their adjoints, and finite-support diagonal
multipliers, together with $D_+$, is a spectral triple.  Its zeta abscissa is
\[
 q_{\pred}=\limsup_{R\to\infty}
 \frac{\log N(R)}{\log R},
 \qquad N(R)=\#\{[w]:\Lambda([w])\le R\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $M_\sigma$ be the supremum of the cardinalities of the fibres of the
map $[w]\mapsto[\sigma w]$.  For every finitely supported vector $\xi$,
Cauchy--Schwarz on each fibre gives
\[
 \|S_\sigma\xi\|^2
 =\sum_u\left|\sum_{[w]:[\sigma w]=u}\xi_{[w]}\right|^2
 \le M_\sigma\sum_{[w]}|\xi_{[w]}|^2.
\]
Hence $S_\sigma$ extends boundedly with
$\|S_\sigma\|\le\sqrt{M_\sigma}$.  On basis vectors,
\[
 [D_+,S_\sigma]e_{[w]}
 =\bigl(\Lambda([\sigma w])-\Lambda([w])\bigr)e_{[\sigma w]},
\]
which is bounded by hypothesis.  The adjoints obey the same estimate.
For each $R$, every class in the length ball has a representative word of
length at most $R-1$, and there are finitely many such words; hence all spectral
projections of $D_+$ on bounded intervals have finite rank and the resolvent is
compact.

For the abscissa, let $q$ denote the displayed limsup.  If $s>q$, choose
$\epsilon>0$ with $s>q+2\epsilon$.  For all large $R$,
$N(R)\le R^{q+\epsilon}$.  Dyadic decomposition gives
\[
 \sum_{[w]}\Lambda([w])^{-s}
 \le C+\sum_{k\ge k_0}N(2^{k+1})2^{-ks}
 \le C+2^{q+\epsilon}\sum_{k\ge k_0}
 2^{-k(s-q-\epsilon)}<\infty.
\]
If $s<q$, choose $\epsilon>0$ with $s+\epsilon<q$ and a sequence $R_j\to\infty$
with $N(R_j)\ge R_j^{s+\epsilon}$.  The partial zeta sum over the length ball is
at least $N(R_j)R_j^{-s}\ge R_j^\epsilon\to\infty$.  Thus the abscissa is $q$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Order and positivity boundary}

Define $a\preceq b$ in the predictive monoid if $b=ca$ for some accumulated
channel $c$.  This is a preorder.  Quotienting by
$a\approx b\Longleftrightarrow(a\preceq b\ \&\ b\preceq a)$ gives a partial
order.  On a component where $\Lambda$ is constant on $\approx$-classes and
strictly increases under strict divisibility, it descends to an intrinsic time
function.  This proves item \textup{(v)} of \Cref{thm:predictive-geometry}.

For item \textup{(vi)}, let $\mathfrak B_+$ be the irreducibly represented
positive renewal algebra.  A functorially extracted fundamental symmetry must
commute with $\mathfrak B_+$.  Since $\mathfrak B_+'=\C\one$, it is scalar;
self-adjointness and $J^2=\one$ give $J=\pm\one$, hence a definite form.
More generally, suppose a diagonal involution satisfies
$JS_e=c(e)S_eJ$ with $c(e)\in\{\pm1\}$.  Writing $Je_x=\eta(x)e_x$ gives
\[
  c(e:x\to y)=\eta(y)\eta(x).
\]
Thus the compatibility signs are an edgewise $\Ztwo$ cochain.  Changing local
sign conventions changes it by a vertex coboundary.  A nonconstant compatible
involution therefore contains precisely the extra signed information later
realized by the double cover.  Combining the preceding lemmas proves every item
of \Cref{thm:predictive-geometry}.

\subsection{Determinant orientation}

At each predictive vertex let $R_x^0$ be the real contrast space of the retained

\subsection{Minimal principal cover}

\begin{lemma}[Cone-positive minimal enrichment]
\label{lem:app-minimal-cover}
Over a fixed connected graph $G$, the gauge classes of objectwise-minimal,
cone-preserving, fibrewise-indefinite local enrichments are in bijection with
$H^1(G;\Ztwo)$.  Every such enrichment has fibre rank two.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fibrewise indefiniteness requires at least one positive and one negative local
line, so the rank is at least two.  Rank two is realized by a signed double
cover.  A unitary carrying one distinguished simplicial renewal cone onto
another permutes its extremal rays: it maps each extremal ray to an extremal
ray, and unitarity makes the positive scalar on that ray equal to one.  Thus in
rank two every edge transport is either the identity or the sheet swap
$S=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{psmallmatrix}$.

Choose sheet labels at the vertices and write the edge transport as
$S^{c(e)}$, $c(e)\in\F_2$.  A change of sheet label $a:V\to\F_2$ sends
$c\mapsto c+\delta a$.  Since a graph has no two-cells, every one-cochain is a
cocycle.  Therefore the gauge classes are
$C^1(G;\F_2)/\delta C^0(G;\F_2)=H^1(G;\Ztwo)$.  The representative $\chi$
constructs the cover
\[
  G^\chi:
  (x,\eta)\xrightarrow{e}(y,\eta\chi(e)),
  \qquad \eta\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
The sheet-sign operator $J_\chi e_{(x,\eta)}=\eta e_{(x,\eta)}$ is a
self-adjoint involution with one positive and one negative line in every fibre.
\end{proof}

Because $[\chi]\ne0$ and $G$ is connected, the monodromy map
$\pi_1(G)\to\Ztwo$ is surjective, so $G^\chi$ is connected.

\subsection{Amplitude lifts}

A connected finite graph is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of

\[
 H^1(G;\Zfour)\cong(\Zfour)^{b_1(G)},
 \qquad
 H^1(G;\Ztwo)\cong(\Ztwo)^{b_1(G)}.

Reduction modulo two is coordinatewise surjective, so every signed class has an

\subsection{The pressure root}

\begin{lemma}[Irreducible integer matrix at spectral radius one]
\label{lem:app-rho-one-cycle}
Let $A$ be the adjacency matrix of a finite strongly connected directed
multigraph.  Then $\rho(A)=1$ if and only if the graph is a single directed
cycle.  Consequently a strongly connected noncycle graph has $\rho(A)>1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A directed cycle has a permutation adjacency matrix, hence spectral radius one.
Conversely, suppose $\rho(A)=1$.  Let $v>0$ be a Perron vector and choose an
index $i$ with $v_i=\min_jv_j$.  Then
\[
 v_i=(Av)_i=\sum_jA_{ij}v_j
 \ge\left(\sum_jA_{ij}\right)v_i.
\]
The row sum is a positive integer because the graph is strongly connected, so
it must equal one, and its unique successor also has the minimum value $v_i$.
Repeating the argument along successors produces a directed cycle all of whose
vertices have outdegree one.  From $i$ there is only this one forward orbit, but
strong connectivity requires $i$ to reach every vertex.  Hence every vertex
lies on that cycle, and the graph is a single directed cycle.
\end{proof}

For the unweighted depth transfer
\[
  B(s)_{xy}=\sum_{e:x\to y}e^{-s\ell_e},
\]
$B(0)=A$, so \Cref{lem:app-rho-one-cycle} and \textup{(P1)} give
$\rho(B(0))>1$.  If $s_2>s_1$, then every supported edge weight is strictly smaller at
$s_2$.  If $v>0$ is a Perron right vector for $B(s_1)$, every row of the
strongly connected support contains a supported edge, and therefore
\[
  B(s_2)v<B(s_1)v=\rho(B(s_1))v
\]
componentwise.  The strict Collatz--Wielandt inequality gives
$\rho(B(s_2))<\rho(B(s_1))$.  The radius is continuous in $s$ and tends to zero
because every entry does.  The intermediate value theorem therefore gives a
unique positive $\beta_{\rm top}$ with $\rho(B(\beta_{\rm top}))=1$.
The identical argument applies to
$K(s)_{xy}=\sum q_e e^{-s\ell_e}$ with $q_e>0$ whenever $\rho(K(0))>1$.

\subsection{Characterization of the modular magnitude}

On the signed recurrent path cover, let

Then

 e^{\beta N}\widetilde S_e e^{-\beta N}

Let $D_0$ be self-adjoint, diagonal in the signed path basis, deck odd, and

\section{Proof of the projective revision algebra}
\label{app:proof-projective-revision}
% =============================================================================

Let $K_{\rev}$ be the elementary abelian two-group of realized reversible
labels acting faithfully on the selected matrix factor $\A_{\rm act}$.  By
\Cref{lem:app-ucp-unit}, every such label acts by a $*$-automorphism.  Every
automorphism of a full complex matrix algebra is inner, so choose unitaries
$R_\alpha$ with $R_0=\one$ and
$\Phi_\alpha=\Ad(R_\alpha)$.

For $\alpha,\beta\in K_{\rev}$, the unitaries
$R_\alpha R_\beta$ and $R_{\alpha+\beta}$ implement the same automorphism.
Their ratio is central, hence scalar:
\[
 R_\alpha R_\beta=\sigma(\alpha,\beta)R_{\alpha+\beta}.
\]
Associativity of the unitary product gives
\[
 \sigma(\alpha,\beta)\sigma(\alpha+\beta,\gamma)
 =\sigma(\beta,\gamma)\sigma(\alpha,\beta+\gamma),
\]
so $\sigma$ is a normalized two-cocycle.  Rephasing $R_\alpha$ changes it by a
coboundary.

The commutator ratio
\[
 b(\alpha,\beta)
 :=\sigma(\alpha,\beta)\sigma(\beta,\alpha)^{-1}
\]
is a bicharacter because $K_{\rev}$ is abelian.  Since every element has order
two,
$b(\alpha,\beta)^2=b(2\alpha,\beta)=1$, and
$b(\alpha,\alpha)=1$.  Hence
$b(\alpha,\beta)=(-1)^{\omega_{\spat}(\alpha,\beta)}$ for a unique alternating
bilinear form $\omega_{\spat}$ over $\F_2$.

The signed sheet action gives
\[
 J_\chi R_\alpha
 =(-1)^{\langle[\chi],\alpha\rangle}R_\alpha J_\chi.
\]
Defining $R_{\alpha+\epsilon t}=R_\alpha J_\chi^\epsilon$ and moving
$J_\chi^\epsilon$ past $R_\beta$ yields
\[
 \omega((\alpha,\epsilon),(\beta,\delta))
 =\omega_{\spat}(\alpha,\beta)
 +\epsilon\langle[\chi],\beta\rangle
 +\delta\langle[\chi],\alpha\rangle.
\]
This proves the first three items of \Cref{thm:projective-revision}.

It remains to identify the primitive algebra.  Let $H$ be the full finite
$\F_2$ label space and let $R=\rad\omega$.  A homogeneous basis element $u_r$
of the abstract twisted group algebra $\C_\sigma[H]$ is central exactly when
$r\in R$.  Fixing a character of this centre reduces the algebra to the twisted
group algebra of $H/R$ with its nondegenerate induced form.  Put
$\dim_{\F_2}(H/R)=2m$ and choose a symplectic basis
$p_1,q_1,\ldots,p_m,q_m$.  After scalar rephasing, the corresponding generators
may be chosen with
\[
 U_j^2=V_j^2=\one,
 \qquad U_jV_j=-V_jU_j,
\]
and generators belonging to distinct pairs commuting.  Each pair generates
$M_2(\C)$, and multiplication gives a surjective homomorphism
\[
 M_2(\C)^{\otimes m}\longrightarrow\C_\sigma[H/R].
\]
Both algebras have complex dimension $2^{2m}$, so this map is an isomorphism:
\[
 \C_\sigma[H/R]\cong M_{2^m}(\C)\cong\Cl_{2m}(\C).
\]
The radical, the quotient, and the matrix type are determined by the derived
commutator form, not separately specified.  This completes the proof.

\section{Proof of the minimal external core}
\label{app:proof-external-core}
% =============================================================================

Let $(H,\omega)=(H_{\rm prim},\omega)$ be nondegenerate of dimension $2m$ and
write $H=K\oplus\F_2t$ as in \textup{(P7)}.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical temporal partner]
\label{lem:app-temporal-partner}
There is a unique nonzero $r\in K$ such that
$\rad(\omega|_K)=K^\perp=\F_2r$.  It satisfies $\omega(t,r)=1$.  Moreover
\[
 K_0:=K\cap t^\perp
\]
is nondegenerate of dimension $2m-2$, and
\[
  H=\Span\{t,r\}\perp K_0,
  \qquad K=\F_2r\oplus K_0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $K$ is a hyperplane, $\dim K^\perp=1$; let $0\ne r\in K^\perp$.  If
$r\notin K$, then $H=K\oplus\F_2r$ and $r$ is orthogonal to both summands,
contradicting nondegeneracy.  Thus $r\in K$ and
$K\cap K^\perp=\F_2r$.  If $\omega(t,r)=0$, then $r$ is orthogonal to $K$ and
to $t$, again a contradiction; over $\F_2$ the nonzero value is one.

For $k\in K$, set $k_0=k+\omega(t,k)r$.  Then $k_0\in K_0$, so
$K=\F_2r\oplus K_0$.  If $x\in K_0$ is orthogonal to $K_0$, then it is also
orthogonal to $r$ because $r\in\rad(\omega|_K)$ and to $t$ by definition.
Hence $x$ is orthogonal to all of $H$ and is zero.  This proves nondegeneracy and
the orthogonal decomposition.
\end{proof}

By \textup{(P7)}, $K_0\ne0$.  Choose $0\ne b\in K_0$.  Nondegeneracy gives
$c\in K_0$ with $\omega(b,c)=1$.  The planes
$P_t=\Span\{t,r\}$ and $P_s=\Span\{b,c\}$ are hyperbolic and orthogonal, so
\[
 W_{\ext}=P_t\perp P_s
\]
is nondegenerate of dimension four.  Since $t\notin K$ and $r,b,c\in K$,
\[
 W_{\ext}\cap K=\Span\{r,b,c\}
\]
has dimension three.  The orthogonal complement
$H_{\intr}=W_{\ext}^\perp$ is nondegenerate of dimension $2m-4$ and lies in
$K_0$: if $x=k+\epsilon t\in W_{\ext}^\perp$, then
$0=\omega(x,r)=\epsilon$, and $0=\omega(x,t)$ then puts $x\in K_0$.
Thus
\[
 H=W_{\ext}\perp H_{\intr},
 \qquad
 K=(W_{\ext}\cap K)\oplus H_{\intr}.
\]

The restrictions of $\omega$ to the two summands are nondegenerate.  The proof
of \Cref{thm:projective-revision} therefore gives
\[
 \A_{\ext}\cong M_4(\C),
 \qquad
 \A_{\intr}\cong M_{2^{m-2}}(\C).
\]
Orthogonality makes their homogeneous generators commute.  Every label has a
unique decomposition $w+v$, so multiplication gives a surjective map
$\A_{\ext}\otimes\A_{\intr}\to\A_{\rm act}$.  The dimensions agree:
\[
 16\cdot 2^{2m-4}=2^{2m}=\dim_\C\A_{\rm act},
\]
hence the map is an isomorphism.

Normalize $R_t=J_\chi$, $R_r$, $R_b$, and $R_c$ as Hermitian involutions.
The only nontrivial commutators among the two hyperbolic pairs are
$R_tR_r=-R_rR_t$ and $R_bR_c=-R_cR_b$.  Then
\[
 \gamma^0:=R_tR_r
\]
is anti-Hermitian and has square $-\one$.  An explicit compatible spatial
Clifford family is
\[
 \gamma^1=R_tR_b,
 \qquad
 \gamma^2=R_tR_c,
 \qquad
 \gamma^3=iR_tR_bR_c.
\]
Each is Hermitian, each squares to $\one$, they anticommute pairwise, and each
anticommutes with $\gamma^0$; these identities follow directly by moving the
four generators past one another with the two displayed signs.  Thus the
external factor is a $(1,3)$ Clifford module.

Finally, $\dim K=2m-1$ is odd.  If $m=1$, then $\dim K=1$ and
$\bigwedge^2K=0$, so there is no nonzero spatial two-reset sector.  The next
case is $m=2$, where $\dim K=3$ and the above construction gives the full
external factor.  Hence three is the minimal spatially nondegenerate rank,
while $H_{\intr}$ may remain nonzero.  This proves
\Cref{thm:external-core}.

\begin{proof}[Detailed proof of \Cref{thm:octahedral-record}]
Because each $\gamma^i$ is a Hermitian involution,
$P_{i,\pm}=(\one\pm\gamma^i)/2$ are orthogonal projections and
$P_{i,+}+P_{i,-}=\one$.  Hence
\[
 \sum_{i,\pm}K_{i,\pm}^*K_{i,\pm}
 =\frac13\sum_i(P_{i,+}+P_{i,-})=\one,
\]
so the six branch maps form a trace-preserving resolved instrument.

Let $p_{i,\pm}(\rho)=\Tr(\rho E_{i,\pm})$.  Since
$\theta_{i,\pm}\theta_{i,\pm}^{\mathsf T}=e_ie_i^{\mathsf T}$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{i,\pm}p_{i,\pm}(\rho)
 \theta_{i,\pm}\theta_{i,\pm}^{\mathsf T}
 &=\sum_i\Tr\!\left(\rho\frac{P_{i,+}+P_{i,-}}3\right)e_ie_i^{\mathsf T}\\
 &=\frac13\sum_i e_ie_i^{\mathsf T}=\frac13I_3.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the second moment is state independent and positive definite.

Write $\gamma(\theta_{i,\pm})=\pm\gamma^i$.  The two signs cancel in
$\gamma(\theta)(\theta\cdot\xi)$, and therefore
\[
 \sum_{i,\pm}p_{i,\pm}(\rho)
 \gamma(\theta_{i,\pm})(\theta_{i,\pm}\cdot\xi)
 =\frac13\sum_i\gamma^i\xi_i.
\]
With frame normalization three, the symbol is the one stated in the theorem.
Using $(\gamma^0)^2=-\one$, $(\gamma^i)^2=\one$, and all mixed
anticommutators equal to zero,
\[
 \left[i\left(\gamma^0\xi_0+\sum_i\gamma^i\xi_i\right)\right]^2
 =\left(\xi_0^2-|\vec\xi|^2\right)\one.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the flat continuum theorem}
\label{app:proof-flat-limit}
% =============================================================================

Put $A^i=\gamma^0\gamma^i$.  The Clifford relations give
$(A^i)^*=A^i$ and
\[
 \{A^i,A^j\}=2\delta^{ij}\one.
\]
For a direction $\theta\in\Sphere^2$, write
$A(\theta)=\theta_iA^i$.

\subsection{Symbol and frame equivalence}

Let $v_i=\kappa M^{ij}\xi_j$.  Since the tensor $v_iv_j$ is symmetric,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sigma_M(\xi)^2
 &=-\left(\gamma^0\xi_0+\gamma^iv_i\right)^2\\
 &=-\left((\gamma^0)^2\xi_0^2
 +\tfrac12\{\gamma^i,\gamma^j\}v_iv_j\right)\\
 &=\left(\xi_0^2-|v|^2\right)\one
 =\left(\xi_0^2-\kappa^2\xi^{\mathsf T}M^2\xi\right)\one.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Because $M\succ0$, the spatial quadratic form is positive definite, giving
signature $(1,3)$.

Set $V_M=\kappa M$ and define
\[
 (U_M\psi)(y)=(\det V_M)^{1/2}\psi(V_My).
\]
The change $x=V_My$ proves unitarity.  Since $V_M$ is symmetric,
\[
 U_M\left[\kappa A^iM^{ij}(-i\partial_{x_j})\right]U_M^{-1}
 =A^i(-i\partial_{y_i}).
\]
Thus constant anisotropy is removed by a spatial linear isometry.

\subsection{Discrete multipliers and common-core convergence}

For mesh $h_R$, define
\[
 s_{\theta,R}(\xi)=h_R^{-1}\sin(h_R\theta\cdot\xi),
 \qquad
 P_{\theta,R}=-i\frac{T_{h_R\theta}-T_{-h_R\theta}}{2h_R}.
\]
Let the finite direction law be
$\nu_R=\sum_a p_a^{(R)}\delta_{\theta_a^{(R)}}$ with second moment $M_R$.
The discrete Hamiltonian is the self-adjoint Fourier multiplier
\[
 \Ham_R=\kappa\sum_a p_a^{(R)}A(\theta_a^{(R)})P_{\theta_a^{(R)},R},
\]
with Hermitian symbol
\[
 h_R(\xi)=\kappa\sum_a p_a^{(R)}A(\theta_a^{(R)})
 s_{\theta_a^{(R)},R}(\xi).
\]
The bounds
$|s_{\theta,R}(\xi)|\le|\theta\cdot\xi|\le|\xi|$ and
$s_{\theta,R}(\xi)\to\theta\cdot\xi$ give
\[
 h_R(\xi)\longrightarrow
 h_M(\xi):=\kappa A^iM^{ij}\xi_j
\]
pointwise whenever $M_R\to M$.  Moreover $\|h_R(\xi)\|\le C|\xi|$ uniformly.
For $u\in\Sch(\R^3,V)$, Plancherel and dominated convergence therefore give
\[
 \|\Ham_Ru-\Ham_Mu\|_2^2
 =\int\|(h_R-h_M)\widehat u\|^2\,\dd\xi\longrightarrow0.
\]
For completeness, on $L^2(\R^{1+3},V)$ define
\[
 \Dirac_R
 =\gamma^0\frac{T_{h_Re_0}-T_{-h_Re_0}}{2h_R}
 +\kappa\sum_a p_a^{(R)}\gamma(\theta_a^{(R)})
       \frac{T_{h_R\theta_a^{(R)}}-T_{-h_R\theta_a^{(R)}}}{2h_R},
\]
where the spatial translations act only in the last three coordinates, and
let
$\Dirac_M=\gamma^0\partial_0+\kappa\gamma^iM^{ij}\partial_j$.
Its Fourier multiplier is obtained from the preceding one by adjoining
$i\gamma^0h_R^{-1}\sin(h_R\xi_0)$.  The same pointwise convergence and the
uniform bound $C|\xi|$ give
$\Dirac_Ru\to\Dirac_Mu$ for every $u\in\Sch(\R^{1+3},V)$ by Plancherel and
dominated convergence.  This is the asserted common-core spacetime
convergence; no resolvent statement for the hyperbolic covariant operator is
used.

The limit Hamiltonian is self-adjoint on $H^1(\R^3,V)$: its Hermitian symbol
squares to $\kappa^2|M\xi|^2\one$, which is comparable to $|\xi|^2$ because
$M\succ0$.  Schwartz space is a core.  Every $\Ham_R$ is self-adjoint as a
Hermitian Fourier multiplier.  Applying \Cref{lem:app-core-resolvent} gives
strong-resolvent convergence, compact-time propagator convergence, and strong
$C_0$ functional-calculus convergence.

\subsection{Fixed-band norm-resolvent estimate}

Let $P_\Lambda$ be the Fourier projection to $|\xi|\le\Lambda$.  The elementary
bound $|\sin t-t|\le|t|^3/6$ gives
\[
 \sup_{|\xi|\le\Lambda}\|h_R(\xi)-h_M(\xi)\|
 \le C\left(\frac16h_R^2\Lambda^3+
 \|M_R-M\|\Lambda\right)=:\varepsilon_R(\Lambda).
\]
For $z\notin\R$, the matrix resolvent identity and Hermiticity give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left\|\left[(\Ham_R-z)^{-1}-(\Ham_M-z)^{-1}\right]P_\Lambda\right\|\\
 &\qquad\le |\im z|^{-2}\varepsilon_R(\Lambda)\longrightarrow0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
If $M_R=M$ exactly, the rate is $O(h_R^2)$ on each fixed band.  This proves all
parts of \Cref{thm:flat-limit} and also explains why no global norm-resolvent
claim is made: the estimate is uniform only for bounded momentum.

\section{Proof of the self-averaged flat endpoint}
\label{app:proof-self-averaging}
% =============================================================================

\begin{proof}[Detailed proof of \Cref{cor:self-averaged-flat}]
For each matrix entry, the function
$\theta\mapsto\theta_i\theta_j$ is bounded.  Birkhoff's ergodic theorem gives,
for almost every realization,
\[
 \frac1{m_R}\sum_{a=1}^{m_R}\theta_{a,i}\theta_{a,j}
 \longrightarrow
 \int\theta_i\theta_j\,\dd\zeta_*(\theta).
\]
Thus the empirical second moments converge entrywise, hence in norm, to $M_*$.
The same ergodic theorem applied to a countable dense family of continuous
functions, followed by uniform approximation, gives weak-star convergence of
the empirical measures.  On this probability-one event, the proof of
\Cref{thm:flat-limit} applies pathwise with $M_R\to M_*$.  Therefore the random
Hamiltonians converge in strong-resolvent sense to the deterministic
$\Ham_{M_*}$.  Positive definiteness, not rotational invariance, is the only
load-bearing condition.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of exchange-selected torsion cancellation}
\label{app:proof-torsion-cancellation}
% =============================================================================

\begin{proof}[Detailed proof of \Cref{thm:torsion-cancellation}]
Let $\mathsf s(z,a,b)=(z,b,a)$.  The common-source formula gives
\[
 K_\diamond(\mathsf sx,\mathsf sy)
 =P(z,z')R_{z'}(b')R_{z'}(a')
 =K_\diamond(x,y),
\]
so exchange covariance is a consequence of the two fresh identical Reads.
Let $\pi_ZP=\pi_Z$ and set
\[
 \widetilde\pi(z,a,b)=\pi_Z(z)R_z(a)R_z(b).
\]
Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sum_{z,a,b}\widetilde\pi(z,a,b)
 K_\diamond((z,a,b),(z',a',b'))\\
 &\qquad=R_{z'}(a')R_{z'}(b')
 \sum_z\pi_Z(z)P(z,z')
 \sum_aR_z(a)\sum_bR_z(b)\\
 &\qquad=\pi_Z(z')R_{z'}(a')R_{z'}(b')
 =\widetilde\pi(z',a',b').
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus $\widetilde\pi$ is stationary; it is exchange invariant by its explicit
formula.  On the recurrent support, primitivity makes it the unique stationary
law.  Since the translational defect is exchange odd,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 S&:=\sum_x\widetilde\pi(x)d^{\rm tr}(x)
 =\sum_x\widetilde\pi(\mathsf sx)d^{\rm tr}(\mathsf sx)\\
 &=-\sum_x\widetilde\pi(x)d^{\rm tr}(x)=-S.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Over $\R^3$ this implies $S=0$.  No parity assumption was imposed on the
rotational record, so the same argument yields no rotational cancellation.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the curved Levi--Civita limit}
\label{app:proof-curved-limit}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Metric compatibility from second moments}

For neighbouring points $x,y$, let $T_e$ transport the direction record and
assume $T_eM_xT_e^{\mathsf T}=M_y$.  Define
\[
 U_e=M_y^{-1/2}T_eM_x^{1/2}.
\]
Then
\[
 U_eU_e^{\mathsf T}
 =M_y^{-1/2}T_eM_xT_e^{\mathsf T}M_y^{-1/2}=I.
\]
Orientation preservation gives $\det U_e>0$, hence $U_e\in SO(3)$.  If
$U_i=\exp(-h\gamma_i)$, differentiation of
$U_iU_i^{\mathsf T}=I$ at $h=0$ gives
$\gamma_i^{\mathsf T}=-\gamma_i$.  Thus the induced connection is
$\so(3)$-valued and metric compatible.

\subsection{Plaquette splitting}

Let an elementary developed reset be
$g_i^{(h)}(x)=(R_i(x),he_i(x))\in SO(3)\ltimes\R^3$, with
$R_i=\one-h\gamma_i+O(h^2)$.  The translation part of
$g_j(x+he_i)g_i(x)$ is
\[
 h e_i+h e_j+h^2(\partial_i e_j-\gamma_j e_i)+O(h^3),
\]
whereas the opposite order gives
\[
 h e_j+h e_i+h^2(\partial_j e_i-\gamma_i e_j)+O(h^3).
\]
Their difference is
\[
 h^2T_{ij}+O(h^3),
 \qquad
 T_{ij}=\partial_i e_j-\partial_j e_i+
 \gamma_i e_j-\gamma_j e_i.
\]
Similarly,
\[
 R_j(x+he_i)R_i(x)-R_i(x+he_j)R_j(x)
 =-h^2F_{ij}+O(h^3),
\]
where
$F_{ij}=\partial_i\gamma_j-\partial_j\gamma_i+[\gamma_i,\gamma_j]$.
Thus translation is torsion and rotation is curvature to leading order.

By \Cref{thm:torsion-cancellation}, the stationary mean of the developed
translational coefficient is zero.  Assumption
\Cref{ass:scaling-package}\textup{(A5)} identifies that mean with the
coefficient of $h^2$ in the coarse-grained translational plaquette.  Hence the
stationary translational plaquette is $O(h^3)$.  Comparing with
$h^2T_{ij}+O(h^3)$ forces $T_{ij}=0$.  The rotational term remains
$-h^2F_{ij}+O(h^3)$ and is unconstrained.

\subsection{Discrete fundamental theorem}

Let $e^a_j$ be the nondegenerate coframe and
$C^a_{ij}=\delta_i e^a_j-\delta_j e^a_i$.  Put
$C_{abc}=\delta_{ad}e_b{}^ie_c{}^jC^d_{ij}$ and define
\[
 \gamma_{d;ae}
 =\frac12(C_{dea}-C_{ade}-C_{ead}).
\]
This is antisymmetric in $a,e$.  Contracting the torsion equation with the
inverse frame gives
$C_{ade}+\gamma_{d;ae}-\gamma_{e;ad}=0$, which the displayed formula satisfies.
If $\gamma'$ is another metric-compatible torsion-free connection, the
difference $\Delta$ is antisymmetric in its last two indices and obeys
$\Delta_{d;ae}=\Delta_{e;ad}$.  Cycling indices gives
\[
 \Delta_{d;ae}=\Delta_{e;ad}=-\Delta_{e;da}
 =-\Delta_{a;de}=\Delta_{a;ed}=\Delta_{d;ea}
 =-\Delta_{d;ae},
\]
so $\Delta=0$.  Hence the connection is unique.  Because centered differences
approximate derivatives with error $O(h^2)$ for $C_b^4$ coframes, this discrete
connection equals the continuum Levi--Civita spin connection plus $O(h^2)$
locally uniformly.

\subsection{Covariant operator convergence}

Put
\[
 B_R^j(x)=\kappa A^aM_R^{aj}(x),
 \qquad B^j(x)=\kappa A^aM^{aj}(x),
\]
and let
$\Omega_j^{\LC}=\tfrac14\omega^{\LC}_{j,ab}A^aA^b$.  In the half-density
realization define
\[
 \Ham_g^{\LC}
 =\frac12\sum_j\left[
 B^j\bigl(-i(\partial_j+\Omega_j^{\LC})\bigr)
 +\bigl(-i(\partial_j+\Omega_j^{\LC})\bigr)^*B^j
 \right].
\]
Uniform positivity of $M$ gives the elliptic graph estimate
\[
 \|u\|_{H^1}\le C(\|\Ham_g^{\LC}u\|_2+\|u\|_2).
\]
The symmetric first-order essential-self-adjointness input from
\Cref{app:proof-status} makes $C_c^\infty$ a core; adding the bounded real
$V_{\rm ren}$ preserves self-adjointness.

Let $U_{j,R}$ be the midpoint spin links generated by the discrete
Levi--Civita coefficients and define
\[
 \nabla_{j,R}\psi(x)=
 \frac{U_{j,R}(x+\tfrac12h_Re_j)^{-1}\psi(x+h_Re_j)
 -U_{j,R}(x-\tfrac12h_Re_j)\psi(x-h_Re_j)}{2h_R}.
\]
Taylor expansion of the spinor and midpoint exponentials gives, for every
compactly supported smooth $\psi$,
\[
 \nabla_{j,R}\psi
 =(\partial_j+\Omega_j^{\LC})\psi+O_{L^2}(h_R^2).
\]
The order-$h_R^2$ terms in the two transported values agree and cancel in the
central difference; division by $2h_R$ leaves the first truncation error at
order $h_R^2$.  Replacing the discrete connection by
$\omega^{\LC}$ costs another $O(h_R^2)$.

With Weyl symmetrization and the real bounded potential, the discrete
Hamiltonian is
\[
 \Ham_R^{\LC}
 =\frac12\sum_j\left[B_R^j(-i\nabla_{j,R})
 +(-i\nabla_{j,R})^*B_R^j\right]+V_{{\rm ren},R}.
\]
The coefficient and adjoint consistency in \textup{(A2)--(A5)}, the $C_b^4$
bounds, and local uniform potential convergence imply
\[
 \Ham_R^{\LC}u\longrightarrow
 (\Ham_g^{\LC}+V_{\rm ren})u
 \qquad(u\in C_c^\infty).
\]
Every discrete operator is self-adjoint by \textup{(A4)}--\textup{(A5)}, and
$C_c^\infty$ is a core of the limit.  Lemma
\ref{lem:app-core-resolvent} yields strong-resolvent, propagator, and $C_0$
functional-calculus convergence.  The principal symbol is
$B^j\xi_j$ and depends only on $M$; the spin subprincipal term is the uniquely
reconstructed Levi--Civita connection; the only separately retained term is
$V_{\rm ren}$.  This proves all six conclusions of \Cref{thm:curved-limit}.

\section{Proof of the no-overstatement boundary}
\label{app:proof-nonclaims}
% =============================================================================

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:no-overstatement}]
Each listed nonclaim follows either from an explicit countermodel or from a
free datum left by the hypotheses.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)},leftmargin=2.7em]
\item A one-state classical replacement process, or a recurrent directed cycle,
is a valid finite renewal process but violates the coherent/reversible or
branching phase criteria.  Thus universal Lorentzian phase membership does not
follow.

\item Tensor any rank-four external model with an independent primitive
Clifford factor $M_{2^k}(\C)$.  The external construction is unchanged while the
total active factor becomes $M_{2^{k+2}}(\C)$.  Hence the total factor need not
be $M_4(\C)$.

\item Predictive growth is independent of the finite external Clifford fibre.
For example, a finite predictive monoid has growth exponent zero, whereas a
free cancellative commuting monoid on $c$ generators has exponent $c$.  The
external $M_4$ factor can be tensored with either, so exponent four is not
forced.

\item The deterministic product order on $\N^c$ has continuum cone
$[0,\infty)^c$, which has exactly $c$ extreme rays.  A smooth Lorentz cone in
dimension $c\ge3$ has a continuum of null extreme rays.  Linear isomorphisms
preserve extreme rays, so finite product order does not by itself converge to
the round Dirac cone.

\item Let $T$ be a finite primitive transfer commuting with the deck
involution.  Its stationary state is unique; applying the deck involution
produces another stationary state, so uniqueness makes the state deck even.
Every deck-odd expectation, including that of $J_\chi$, vanishes.  Thus the
signed cover does not select an orientation in such a phase.

\item Detailed balance alone does not remove torsion.  On two diamond records
$+,-$, take
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4\\[2pt]1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \pi=(2/3,1/3).
\]
Then $\pi_+P_{+-}=\pi_-P_{-+}=1/6$, so the primitive chain is reversible.  With
$d(+)=v$ and $d(-)=-v$, its mean defect is $v/3\ne0$.  The missing property is
exchange covariance.

\item For the centered difference, consider the positive-measure box
\[
 \mathcal B_h=\{(\pi/h+\eta,\xi_2,\xi_3):
 |\eta|,|\xi_2|,|\xi_3|<\varepsilon\}.
\]
Uniformly on this box,
\[
 h^{-1}\sin(h\xi_1)=-h^{-1}\sin(h\eta)
 =-\eta+O(h^2\varepsilon^3),
 \qquad
 h^{-1}\sin(h\xi_j)=\xi_j+O(h^2\varepsilon^3)
 \quad(j=2,3),
\]
so the discrete Dirac symbol remains $O(\varepsilon)$, whereas the continuum
symbol has modulus $\pi h^{-1}+O(\varepsilon)$.  Choose $\varepsilon>0$ small.
For every fixed nonreal $z$, the discrete resolvent symbol on $\mathcal B_h$
is then bounded below in norm by a positive constant, while the continuum
resolvent symbol tends uniformly to zero.  The essential supremum of the
multiplier difference therefore stays bounded below by a positive constant.
Hence the global operator-norm resolvent difference cannot tend to zero.

\item Any two distinct smooth uniformly positive fields, for example
$M_1(x)=I$ and $M_2(x)=(1+\varepsilon f(x))I$ with bounded nonconstant smooth
$f$ and sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, satisfy the analytic class but yield
different metrics.  The local finite hypotheses also contain no gluing datum
fixing global topology.

\item The curved theorem accepts an open class of $C_b^4$ coframes.  A generic
member does not satisfy any prescribed Einstein equation.  Since the proof uses
only metric compatibility, torsion closure, and operator convergence, it cannot
imply a curvature field equation absent from the hypotheses.

\item The finite external Clifford and renewal data contain no representation
labels or coupling laws specifying matter multiplets, gauge groups, measurement
dynamics, horizon entropy, or cosmological evolution.  Models with the same
kinematic data and different added matter sectors therefore satisfy the same
flagship hypotheses.

\item Multiplying every conventional unit of time by a positive constant changes
the numerical lapse and propagation speed but no dimensionless pressure equation
or operator-isomorphism statement.  Therefore an absolute number of seconds or
energy units cannot be obtained without metrological calibration.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
